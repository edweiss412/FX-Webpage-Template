# Manual-sync emission gaps and the broken existing-show retry

**Status:** APPROVED — cross-model adversarial review converged APPROVE / 0 findings at round 9 (2026-08-14; rounds and dispositions in `docs/review-rounds/fix/sync-observability-gaps/04f601134519.md`)
**Arc:** `fix/sync-observability-gaps`
**Closes:** `BL-MANUAL-SYNC-UNEMITTED`, `BL-PENDING-RETRY-EXISTING-SHOW-THROWS` (BACKLOG.md)
**Parent design:** `docs/superpowers/specs/observability/2026-08-09-sync-log-show-attribution-design.md` — its §6.1/§6.2 filed both defects and fenced them out of the attribution arc. This spec is the filed follow-up.

## 1. Problem

Two defects, both probed, both filed 2026-08-09:

1. **The existing-show pending-ingestion retry cannot work.** `app/api/admin/pending-ingestions/[id]/retry/route.ts:435-444` calls `runManualSyncForShow_unlocked`, which invokes `processOneFile_unlocked` with five arguments (`lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:286-293`) — never the sixth `prepared` parameter — and `processOneFile_unlocked` throws `SyncInfraError` unconditionally when `prepared` is absent (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3452-3458`). For any non-archived show the retry throws before any sync work; only the archived silent-skip at `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3448-3450` avoids it. A shipped admin action appears to work and cannot.

2. **Manual sync is largely unemitted.** The attribution arc wired a `logSync` sink into every manual entry point, but a sink is necessary, not sufficient:
   - `runManualStageForFirstSeen` reaches its sole emission — `emitSuccessfulPhase2Tail` at `lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:161` — only on the `auto_publish_ready` path. The `stage` (`lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:92-94`), `hard_fail` (`lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:95-97`), `pass` (`lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:98`), phase-2 `stale` (`lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:144-146`), and `defer` (`lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:201-203`) branches return without emitting; the filing probe supplied `logSync`, exercised four of the five, and measured `logSyncCalls: 0` on every one exercised. Thrown phase-1/phase-2 failures escape through the unguarded awaits at `lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:228` (`runPhase1`) and `lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:128` (`runPhase2`).
   - `runManualSyncForShow` awaits `runOne` with no catch (`lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:430`); a probe with `processDeps.logSync` installed and `runOne` throwing produced `{"thrown":"probe-prepare-failure","logSyncCalls":0}`.
   - The live staged-apply path writes no attempt row on ANY outcome. `applyStaged_unlocked` invokes the tail with only a tx-bound `upsertAdminAlert` (`lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1492-1500`) — no sink, no start — and neither live caller supplies tail deps (`app/api/admin/staged/[fileId]/apply/route.ts:158`, `app/api/admin/show/staged/[stagedId]/apply/route.ts:170`; both carry the `sync-log-emission-gap` marker). An existing-show staged apply never even reaches the tail — the invocation at `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1474` is gated on `autoPublishFirstSeen`.

Consequence: Doug's most deliberate actions — approving a staged parse, retrying a pending ingestion, manually re-syncing a show — are invisible to `observe synclog`, while cron's routine passes are fully recorded.

### 1.1 Resolved scope — do not relitigate

- **The retry-route mechanism choice (prepare-in-route vs locked entry point) is an internal design call delegated to this spec** by `BL-PENDING-RETRY-EXISTING-SHOW-THROWS` ("decide whether the route should call `prepareProcessOneFile` itself, or use the locked `runManualSyncForShow` entry point"). §3.1 decides it. It is not user-visible and needs no product escalation.
- **In-lock `sync_log` emission is the ratified pattern, not an invariant-10 violation — and it has TWO writer families with different transaction topology (R6 F1).** (i) The **injected-sink family**: `logSync`-helper emissions through `deps.logSync` (the direct calls span `processOneFile_unlocked`'s body from the drift-recheck skip at `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3469` onward, plus the applied tail's at `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2485`); in production the injected sink is `writeSyncLog` on its own connection (`lib/sync/syncLog.ts:65-72`), so these rows commit independently of the held tx. (ii) The **recovery-sink family**: `tx.insertSyncLog` on the held transaction itself (`PostgresPipelineTx.insertSyncLog`, `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:1242-1243`; recovery arms at `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2713`, `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2749`; manual twins at `lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:172`, `lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:218`) — these ROLL BACK with an aborted tx, which is exactly why the §3.1/§3.3 tracker counts only family (i). The attribution design explicitly kept the sink write inside the cron transaction scope (parent spec §7; §6 first bullet accepts the NULL-attribution cost). The shared pipeline's dark terminal branches (all inside or below `handleFetchFailure_unlocked`, plus the override-TOCTOU skip) are a defect this spec repairs (§3.5), not part of the ratified pattern; the §3.5 no-show/existing-pending repairs join family (ii), matching their sibling arms, while §3.2's new emissions join family (i). Invariant 10's POST-COMMIT rule governs admin-alert / `app_events` / `logAdminOutcome` emits, not this channel.
- **The archived and finalize-owned refusals stay silent.** DEF-3/DEF-4/DEF-5 ratified "no mutation, no fetch, no log" for archived-show refusals (`lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:311-313`, `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3446-3450`, `app/api/admin/pending-ingestions/[id]/retry/route.ts:408-411`). This spec adds no emission to any blocked/refusal branch.
- **The wizard staged-apply path attempts no sync and gets no emission.** Ratified in the parent spec §6.2 (R11 F1 / R12 F1): `sourceScope: "wizard"` stages approval and manifest metadata only; a gap marker or an emission there would falsely record a sync attempt.
- **`duration_ms` measures the runner boundary, not the operator's click.** Parent spec §3.3 documents that route-level preparation is excluded from the measured window. §3.4's staged-apply start (captured at `applyStaged` entry) has the same shape and the same documented limit.
- **Sink mechanics are settled.** Show attribution resolves at the sink via subselect (`lib/sync/syncLog.ts:43-50`); `status` is free text (`supabase/migrations/20260501001000_internal_and_admin.sql:225` — `status text not null`, no CHECK), so new outcome/reason strings need no migration. Duration derives in the `logSync` helper (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2303-2309`) with the absent-start → NULL and non-monotonic → clamp-0 guards already ratified.
- **Escaped-throw rows carry NULL duration by design** (parent spec §3.3.1: "The attempt aborted; a partial elapsed time would misreport as a completed duration"). §3.2/§3.3's thrown-attempt emissions follow it.

## 2. Goal and acceptance posture

Every manual sync attempt that reaches a terminal outcome writes exactly one `sync_log` row, or the failure to write it is surfaced — never silently absent. The pending-ingestion retry of an existing show executes real sync work.

**Consequence bound (for review):** every attempt is recorded correctly or its recording failure is signaled; a conservative non-emission on a ratified silent branch (§1.1 refusals, wizard path) is a DOCUMENTED LIMIT, not a finding. **Threat model:** accidental emission gaps introduced by ordinary code evolution. A **new outcome branch** on the first-seen stage path is covered structurally (the two §3.2 `never`-checks); on the staged-apply path, non-applied outcomes are ratified non-emission (§3.4/§6), so a new non-applied variant inherits that limit rather than a guard; on the fetch-failure recovery surface the cover is the §3.5 partition matrix — a regression pin over the modeled partition, with arms outside it falling to the filed derived guard (§3.5/§6). A **new caller** is covered where the production default sits on its path: §3.4's chokepoint covers every caller of the locked `applyStaged` wrapper (both live routes and `applyStagedParse`, `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:2101-2105`; `applyStaged_unlocked`'s only production callers are the wrapper's own lock closures, `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1874`, `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1922`). A future caller that bypasses a covered chokepoint — a direct `applyStaged_unlocked` call, or a manual-runner call omitting the injected sink — is the class the parent arc filed as its derived writer-reachability guard (BACKLOG.md `BL-SYNC-LOG-ATTRIBUTION-METATEST`, referenced from `tests/sync/manualSyncInstallsSink.test.ts`) — out of scope here (§7), stated as a limit (§6). Adversarial DB manipulation and operator misuse are out of scope.

### Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1 (retry works).** A pending-ingestion retry against an existing, non-archived, non-finalize-owned show reaches `processOneFile_unlocked` with a `prepared` value and completes with a real `ProcessOneFileResult` — no `SyncInfraError("processOneFile_unlocked", ...)`. Verified with a live retry against a seeded existing show (the existing tests inject `processOneFile_unlocked`, which is why the defect never surfaced).
- **AC-2 (first-seen stage emits).** Every terminal `RunManualStageForFirstSeenResult` outcome writes exactly one `sync_log` row: `applied` via the existing tail, every other outcome via the §3.2 single-site emit. Rows written through the helper carry non-NULL `duration_ms`.
- **AC-3 (throws emit).** A thrown phase-1/phase-2 failure inside `runManualStageForFirstSeen`, a thrown `runOne` inside `runManualSyncForShow`, a thrown `runUnlocked` inside `runManualSyncForShow_unlocked` (the retry route's direct path — §3.1), and a thrown live apply inside `applyStaged` (§3.4's closure table) each write one row with `outcome: "parse_error"`, `code: classifySyncFailure(error)`, `payload: errorPayload(error)`, NULL duration — **only when no row was already written for the attempt** (§3.2's tracked-sink dedupe) — and then rethrow **the original error, always**: the catch-emit is itself guarded, and a sink failure there is escalated under `SYNC_LOG_EMIT_FAILED` (`log.error`, app_events-only forensic code), never substituted for the original throw.
- **AC-4 (staged apply emits).** A committed live staged apply — first-seen AND existing-show — writes exactly one `sync_log` row with `outcome: "applied"`, non-NULL `duration_ms`, the apply's `parseWarnings`, and (being post-commit) a resolved `show_id`.
- **AC-5 (no double emission).** No path writes two rows for one attempt: the §3.2 single-site emit skips `applied` (the tail owns it); the staged-apply tail invocation stays sinkless (§3.4 owns the applied row at its post-commit site); and all three tracked catch-emits (§3.2, §3.3, and the `_unlocked` runner's) fire only when the attempt's tracked sink recorded no prior row — a failure thrown AFTER an outcome row landed (e.g. in the post-tail quality/alert steps) adds nothing and rethrows.
- **AC-6 (scoped).** AC-2 through AC-4 govern the surfaces this spec touches. The §1.1 ratified-silent branches and the §6 limits are excluded by name, not by omission.
- **AC-7 (shared-branch sweep).** Every cell of `handleFetchFailure_unlocked`'s modeled input partition (code class × show presence × existing-pending, §3.5) writes exactly one row; on the arms this arc repairs, the recorded code equals the arm's RETURNED terminal code (the carve cells record `SYNC_FILE_FAILED`); the pre-existing emitting arms keep their ratified recovery-row shape (`outcome: "error"`, §3.5). The under-lock pull-sheet-override skip writes its skip row.
- **AC-8 (first-seen prepare failures recorded).** A `prepareFirstSeenStage` failure on the retry route's first-seen branch writes one `parse_error` row carrying the `FirstSeenStagePrepareError` code while the typed 502/409 response is unchanged (§3.1).

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Retry route: prepare in-route, thread `prepared` through

**Decision: the route calls `prepareProcessOneFile` itself, inside its held row lock, and `runManualSyncForShow_unlocked` gains a required `prepared` parameter that it forwards.**

Mechanism:

- `runManualSyncForShow_unlocked(tx, driveFileId, mode, fileMeta, deps, prepared)` — new **sixth, required** parameter of type `PreparedProcessOneFile` (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2944-2999`), forwarded as the sixth argument at `lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:286-293`. Required, not optional: an optional parameter re-creates the shipped defect for the next caller; required makes the omission a compile error (the precedent is `ProcessOneFileResult.applied.parseWarnings`, made required exactly so a future caller "cannot silently drop the channel", `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:427-431`). The route's injectable signature at `app/api/admin/pending-ingestions/[id]/retry/route.ts:59-65` widens to match.
- In the route's existing-show branch, after the watched-folder check (`app/api/admin/pending-ingestions/[id]/retry/route.ts:431-434`): `const prepared = await prepareProcessOneFile(row.drive_file_id, "manual", metadata, processDeps)` with the same `processDeps` object the sync call receives (`{ logSync: writeSyncLog }`), then pass `prepared` to `runManualSyncForShowUnlocked`. The call goes through a new injectable route dep (`LivePendingIngestionRouteDeps.prepareProcessOneFile?`, defaulted to the real function in `depsWithDefaults`, `app/api/admin/pending-ingestions/[id]/retry/route.ts:207-231`) — the same seam shape every other collaborator on this route already uses, and what the §5 throwing-prepare test injects.
- **The first-seen prepare failure branches emit too (R4 F1).** `prepareFirstSeenStage` is the first-seen branch's core sync preparation — sheet export, parse, Drive enrichment — and its typed failure returns (`DRIVE_FETCH_FAILED` → 502, `STAGED_PARSE_FAILED` → 409, `app/api/admin/pending-ingestions/[id]/retry/route.ts:495-501`) currently terminate the attempt with no row, the exact class as the existing-show prepare. Repair inside that existing catch, before the typed response: emit `{ driveFileId, outcome: "parse_error", code: <FirstSeenStagePrepareError code>, payload: errorPayload(cause) }`, NULL duration. The typed 502/409 responses are unchanged — this branch keeps its return-not-rethrow shape; only the recording is new. A sink failure here is escalated under `SYNC_LOG_EMIT_FAILED` and the typed response still returns (fail-open — the response contract outranks the recording). **Both route-body catch emissions (this one and the existing-show prepare guard) write through a new injectable route dep `logSyncSink?: typeof writeSyncLog`, defaulted to `writeSyncLog` in `depsWithDefaults` (R7 F2)** — a hardcoded module call would force every route test onto the real DB; the seam matches every other collaborator on this route and is what the §5 route tests inject.
- **The prepare call itself is guarded (R2 F3).** `prepareProcessOneFile` converts fetch/binding failures into `kind: "fetch_failure"`, but not everything: its later preparation work — e.g. `extractSourceAnchors`'s `XLSX.read` and the discard-and-rerun reconcile — sits outside its internal catches and can throw. Relocated into the route, that throw would reach only the outer `PENDING_INGESTION_RETRY_FAILED` guard and write no `sync_log` row, re-creating the exact silence this arc closes. The route therefore wraps the `prepareProcessOneFile` await: on catch, emit `{ driveFileId, outcome: "parse_error", code: classifySyncFailure(error), payload: errorPayload(error) }` through the injectable `logSyncSink` route dep (R7 F2; NULL duration, escaped-throw rule §1.1), then rethrow the original error — the outer guard's 500 behavior is unchanged. No dedupe sentinel is needed: `prepareProcessOneFile` writes no `sync_log` rows itself, so a prepare-throw is always the attempt's first and only row. A sink failure inside this catch follows the §3.2 posture: escalated under `SYNC_LOG_EMIT_FAILED`, original error still thrown.
- **Both injectable seams widen, not one (R2 F4):** `runManualSyncForShow_unlocked`'s own signature AND the `RunManualSyncForShowDeps.processOneFile_unlocked` injection type (`lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:55-61`, currently five-parameter) gain the `prepared` parameter — without the deps-type widening the six-argument forward at `lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:286` is a compile error against injected doubles, and the §5 six-argument spy could not typecheck.
- **The `_unlocked` runner gets its own tracked catch (R3 F1).** The retry route calls `runManualSyncForShow_unlocked` directly, bypassing §3.3's catch (which lives in the locked `runManualSyncForShow` wrapper). A throw inside `processOneFile_unlocked` before its first emission — the deferral recheck (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3474`), override handling (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3515-3530`), phase 1 (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3533`), phase 2, or in-flight recovery work — would otherwise reach only the route's generic outer guard and record nothing. `runManualSyncForShow_unlocked` therefore wraps its `runUnlocked` await with the SAME tracked-sink catch as §3.3: wrap the forwarded `logSync` with the `rowWritten` tracker; on catch, emit `parse_error` via the (separate-connection) sink only when `rowWritten` is false; rethrow the original error unconditionally; a sink failure in the catch escalates under `SYNC_LOG_EMIT_FAILED`. **The tracker observes the injected separate-connection sink ONLY, and that is load-bearing:** the tx-bound recovery writes (`recoveryTx.insertSyncLog`) roll back with the aborted transaction on a later throw, so counting them would suppress the catch-emit for a row that no longer exists. A durable row exists iff the tracked sink fired; the catch-emit keys on exactly that.
- `prepared.kind` dispatch needs no new handling: `processOneFile_unlocked` already handles every kind — `skip`, `asset_recovery`, `revision_race_cooldown`, `revision_race`, `fetch_failure`, `ready` — with an emission per kind (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3480-3506`): the skip/race kinds through the injected-sink family, `fetch_failure` through `handleFetchFailure_unlocked`'s recovery-sink family (§1.1's two-family topology, with the §3.5 repairs closing its dark arms). Threading `prepared` therefore buys the retry path the full cron emission surface for free.

Why in-lock preparation is correct here:

- The route's own first-seen branch already runs its full preparation — sheet export, parse, Drive enrichment — inside the same held lock (`prepareFirstSeenStage` at `app/api/admin/pending-ingestions/[id]/retry/route.ts:497`). The existing-show branch simply gains parity.
- Manual mode takes no watermark skip and no revision-race cooldown inside `prepareProcessOneFile`: the watermark gate is automatic-mode-only (`isAutomaticMode`, `lib/sync/perFileProcessor.ts:58-60`) and cooldown checks are gated on `shouldUseRevisionRaceCooldown(mode)` = cron/push only (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2488-2490`, checked at `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3031` and `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3062`). A manual retry cannot be spuriously skipped by preparation.
- A `captureBinding`/fetch failure does not throw out of `prepareProcessOneFile` — it returns `kind: "fetch_failure"` (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3054-3061`), which `processOneFile_unlocked` routes to `handleFetchFailure_unlocked` (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3497-3506`), the same recovery cron uses.

**Rejected: switching the route to the locked `runManualSyncForShow` entry point.** The route already holds the per-show advisory lock for `driveFileId` via `withRowTryLock` (`app/api/admin/pending-ingestions/[id]/retry/route.ts:400`, wrapping `withPostgresSyncPipelineLock` at `app/api/admin/pending-ingestions/[id]/retry/route.ts:121-126`). `runManualSyncForShow` acquires the same key again on separate connections (`lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:301-302`, blocking `tryOnly: false`) — a second holder on one hashkey, which is precisely the nested-holder deadlock invariant 2's single-holder rule prohibits. Restructuring to release the row lock first would forfeit the `FOR UPDATE` pending-ingestions row guard (`readLockedPendingIngestion`, `app/api/admin/pending-ingestions/[id]/retry/route.ts:237-250`) and the in-tx response reads, for no observability gain. The lock topology stays: the route is the single holder; callees assert (`assertShowLockHeld` at `lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:277`, `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3445`).

### 3.2 `runManualStageForFirstSeen`: one exhaustive emit site

**Decision: emit once, at the function's return boundary, from an exhaustive switch on the result outcome — not per early-return branch.**

The per-branch shape (an emit before each `return` in `toResult`) is the shape that failed: a future branch added without its emit is silent again. A single site after `toResult` resolves, switching exhaustively on `RunManualStageForFirstSeenResult["outcome"]` (`lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:40-59`), makes a new outcome variant a compile error until the mapping says what it records (tsc exhaustiveness via `never`-check). This is the derived-cover lesson from the parent arc — five hand-enumerated covers came up short there (parent spec §3.5.1).

**Exhaustiveness must also hold one layer down (R1 F3).** `toResult` converts `Phase1Result` and currently ends in an implicit `return null`, which the caller coalesces to `{ outcome: "parsed" }` (`lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:245-255`) — so a `Phase1Result` variant this function does not handle (`auto_apply_with_holds` and `shrink_held` exist today, `lib/sync/phase1.ts:160`, `lib/sync/phase1.ts:168`) silently becomes `parsed`, and a FUTURE phase-1 variant would too, defeating the outcome-layer guard from below. `toResult` therefore becomes an exhaustive switch over `Phase1Result["outcome"]` with its own `never`-check and no `null` fallback: `auto_apply_with_holds` and `shrink_held` — both unreachable on a genuine first-seen file (each requires an existing show/prior) — keep today's caller-visible behavior but return the parsed variant carrying a discriminator, `{ outcome: "parsed", degenerate: "unexpected_phase1_outcome" }` (new optional field on that variant, `lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:59`). **`toResult` itself emits nothing** — emission stays at the single outcome-layer site, which reads the discriminator to choose the row's reason, so the two layers cannot double-emit. The `?? { outcome: "parsed" }` coalesce at the call site is deleted with the `null` return.

`logSync` (the module-private helper at `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2284-2311`) is exported so this module reuses the single duration/entry-shape implementation instead of a drifting copy. The emit passes `depsWithStart` (`lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:216-219`, already captured), so every helper-written row carries duration.

Outcome mapping (cron-parity where a cron twin exists):

| Result outcome | Emitted entry | Cron twin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `applied` | **no emit** — `emitSuccessfulPhase2Tail` already wrote it (`lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:161` → `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2485`) | `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3807` |
| `parsed_pending_review` | `{ outcome: "stage", stagedId }` | `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3620-3624` |
| `hard_failed` | `{ outcome: "hard_fail", code: errorCode, showId: null }` (first-seen: no show row exists) | `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3552-3561` |
| `deferred` | `{ outcome: "skipped", reason }` + payload `{ kind: "mi8_debounce_skip", reason }` | `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3625-3631` |
| `parsed` | `{ outcome: "skipped", reason: "first_seen_phase1_pass" }` — or `reason: "first_seen_unexpected_phase1_outcome"` when the result carries the `degenerate` discriminator; payload carries `stagedId` when present | none — see below |

The `parsed` row is the one mapping without a cron twin: phase-1 `pass` means "existing show, clean parse" (`lib/sync/phase1.ts:591`, reachable only when a `shows` row exists, `lib/sync/phase1.ts:587-589`), which on the first-seen stage path is a degenerate no-op — the function stages nothing and applies nothing. Recording it as a skip with a distinct reason keeps the row truthful; `status` is free text, so the new reason strings (`first_seen_phase1_pass`, `first_seen_unexpected_phase1_outcome`) are sink-legal as-is.

The `hard_failed` mapping deliberately absorbs the phase-2 `stale` branch (`lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:144-146`, which returns `hard_failed` carrying `phase2.code`): the sink's `status` column records the code either way (`statusFor`, `lib/sync/syncLog.ts:17-19`), so only the `message` prefix differs from cron's `stale:` row. Fidelity of the returned API is unchanged; one emit site outweighs a cosmetic message-prefix parity.

**Thrown attempts — tracked-sink dedupe (R1 F2).** A throw can escape at two very different moments: before any row exists (a phase-1 explosion), or AFTER an outcome row already landed (the tail wrote `applied` at `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2485`, then `evaluateQualityRegression_unlocked` at `lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:177` or `resolveStaleSyncProblemAlerts_unlocked` at `lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:189` threw). A naive catch-emit would record a second, factually wrong `parse_error` row over a recorded outcome. So the function threads a **tracking wrapper** around the injected sink for the attempt: `depsWithStart.logSync` is replaced by a wrapper that sets a local `rowWritten = true` before delegating, and the catch around the body from the `runPhase1` await through `toResult` (`lib/sync/runManualStageForFirstSeen.ts:228-255`) emits `{ outcome: "parse_error", code: classifySyncFailure(error), payload: errorPayload(error) }` (`classifySyncFailure` at `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2578`, `errorPayload` at `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2339`) **only when `rowWritten` is false**, without the helper's start (direct sink call → NULL duration, §1.1 escaped-throw rule). Cron parity for the no-row case: the file-loop escaped-throw catch at `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:4104-4114`.

**Rethrow posture (R1 F6):** the catch rethrows **the original error, unconditionally**. The catch-emit itself is wrapped: if the sink rejects there, the sink failure is escalated via `log.error` with `code: "SYNC_LOG_EMIT_FAILED"` (app_events-only forensic code, `*_EMIT_FAILED` family precedent at `app/api/admin/pending-ingestions/[id]/retry/route.ts:601`) and the ORIGINAL error still propagates — a recording failure must never displace the failure it was recording. This preserves the route's existing `PENDING_INGESTION_RETRY_FAILED` → 500 behavior byte-for-byte.

**Emission failure posture on the non-thrown paths:** the mapping-table emits run in-lock through the tracked sink, exactly like the existing tail emission on this path; a sink throw there propagates and aborts the attempt (unchanged posture — the tail's `logSync` at `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2485` already behaves this way). No new swallow.

### 3.3 `runManualSyncForShow`: catch the escaped throw

Wrap the `runOne` await (`lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:430-468`) with the same two guards as §3.2:

- **Tracked-sink dedupe (R1 F2).** `runOne` reaches many in-lock emissions before it can throw — every `processOneFile_unlocked` outcome row, the recovery sinks, and (for `applied`) the tail row — and can then still throw in the post-commit steps (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2901-2940`: variant emit, snapshot promote, deferred notice, unlanded renames, role-token emit). The `processDeps.logSync` the function forwards is wrapped with the `rowWritten` tracker; the catch emits `{ driveFileId, outcome: "parse_error", code: classifySyncFailure(error), payload: errorPayload(error) }` (NULL duration) **only when no row was recorded for the attempt**. A post-commit-tail throw after a committed apply therefore adds nothing — the `applied` row already stands, and inventing a `parse_error` over it is exactly the corruption the tracker prevents.
- **Rethrow posture (R1 F6).** The original error is rethrown unconditionally; a catch-emit sink failure is escalated under `code: "SYNC_LOG_EMIT_FAILED"` and never substituted.

This is the manual twin of the cron file-loop catch (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:4104-4114`), minus the escalation alert: cron has no operator watching, so it raises a durable per-show alert (`emitEscapedSyncFailureAlert`, `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:4119-4126`); the manual paths rethrow into a route/action that returns the failure to the operator who just clicked, and duplicating that signal as an alert was considered and rejected.

The catch-emit happens after the lock callback has unwound (the throw already tore it down), on the sink's own connection — no lock is held. The DEF-3 blocked branches and the preflight `ConcurrentSyncSkipped` sentinel (`lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:309-323`) stay silent per §1.1; in-attempt lock contention already emits `CONCURRENT_SYNC_SKIPPED` inside `processOneFile` (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2891-2894`).

### 3.4 Staged apply: one post-commit applied row at the shared chokepoint

**Decision: `applyStaged` itself emits the applied row, post-commit, in the existing live tail region — routes change nothing. The ledger's literal suggestion (routes inject tail deps) is rejected.**

Why not tail-deps injection from the routes:

- `firstPublishedTailDeps` **replaces** the tail's whole deps object (`lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1492`), whose default is the tx-bound `upsertAdminAlert` that writes `SHOW_FIRST_PUBLISHED` through the apply transaction — the R3 fix for the FK-on-uncommitted-show rollback (`lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1487-1500`; "intentionally NOT defaulted here (no tx in scope)", `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:990-991`). A route cannot construct that tx-bound writer, so route injection either clobbers the R3 fix or demands a second seam. The field stays test-injectable only.
- Per-route injection is a hand-enumerated cover — the exact shape that failed five times in the parent arc. A default at the shared chokepoint covers both live routes today and every future caller by construction.
- The tail runs **inside** the apply tx; emitting there writes the attempt row before commit and — for first-seen — permanently NULL-attributed (parent spec §6, first limit). The post-commit region emits after the show row is visible, so the staged path's applied row attributes even at show birth. The existing-show staged apply never reaches the tail at all (`autoPublishFirstSeen` gate, `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1474`), so the tail was never a single point for this surface; the post-commit region is.

Mechanism:

- New optional `ApplyStagedDeps.logSync?: ProcessOneFileDeps["logSync"]`. **The production default is resolved at the emit site in the OUTER `applyStaged`, not in `depsWithDefaults` (R1 F5):** `depsWithDefaults` runs inside `applyLiveWithDriveReverify` (`lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1841`) and its resolved object never returns to `applyStaged`, which still holds the caller's raw deps at `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1952` — a default planted only in `depsWithDefaults` would leave the outer emit sinkless and the whole surface dark. The emit site therefore resolves `deps.logSync ?? writeSyncLog` (`lib/sync/syncLog.ts:65`) directly, the exact pattern the same region already uses for `deps.upsertAdminAlert ?? defaultUpsertAdminAlert` (`lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1991`) and `deps.readLandedSnapshotStatus ?? defaultReadLandedSnapshotStatus` (`lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:2025`). Tests inject a spy; production needs no route changes.
- `applyStaged`'s live branch (`lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1947-1952`) captures `attemptStartedAtMs` from `deps.now` before `applyLiveWithDriveReverify`, mirroring `processOneFile` (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2843-2844`).
- In the live post-commit region, ahead of the existing steps (the same drop-prevention argument that placed the variant emit first, `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1969-1975`): on `result.outcome === "applied"`, call the exported `logSync` helper exactly as the tail does (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2485`): `logSync({ logSync: sink, attemptStartedAtMs, now }, driveFileId, appliedResult, undefined, appliedResult.parseWarnings)` — the helper reads warnings from its separate fifth parameter, not from the result object (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2291`, `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2302`), so omitting it writes an applied row with no warnings (R2 F2). `appliedResult` is the `ProcessOneFileResult`-shaped `{ outcome: "applied", showId, parseWarnings, appliedRoleMappings }`. The emit is wrapped in its own try/catch whose escalation is `log.error` with **`code: "SYNC_LOG_EMIT_FAILED"`** and `source: "sync.applyStaged"` (R1 F7 — the code is named so the "recorded or signaled" half of the consequence bound is provable; the §5 test asserts the code, not merely that some escalation fired). Fail-open: a sink failure must not turn a committed apply into an error response — the region's ratified per-step isolation contract, `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1969-1989`.
- `parseWarnings` is carried out on the applied `ApplyStagedResult` variant (`lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:261-280`) as a **required** field sourced from `coreResult.parseWarnings` at the applied-result build (`lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1450-1459`) — the same tsc-forced pattern that protects the tail (`lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1478-1484`). `appliedRoleMappings` already rides the type (`lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:279`).
- The tail invocation keeps its sinkless deps; its site comment and both routes' `sync-log-emission-gap` markers are rewritten to point here (the gap is closed; the tail's no-op `logSync` on this path becomes a documented non-site, not a filed defect). **"Sinkless" is enforced at runtime, typed as intent (R4 F2, corrected R7 F1):** `firstPublishedTailDeps` currently accepts the tail's complete deps type INCLUDING `logSync` (declared at `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:373` and `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:954`, threaded at `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:992`, passed verbatim at `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1492`), so a caller supplying a sink there would make the tail emit an applied row in-tx and §3.4 emit a second one post-commit. Two layers close it: (1) the field's type narrows to `Omit<Parameters<typeof emitSuccessfulPhase2Tail>[0]["deps"], "logSync">` at both declarations — this rejects fresh object literals carrying `logSync` but, TypeScript being structural, does NOT stop a wider aliased value, so it is intent, not the guarantee; (2) the guarantee is the invocation site: the tail call at `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1492` destructures `logSync` out of whatever it received (`const { logSync: _stripped, ...tailDeps } = ...`) and passes only the rest, so the staged tail is sinkless at runtime regardless of what a caller smuggles through the type. A §5 test injects `firstPublishedTailDeps` WITH a sink (cast through the wider type) and asserts the tail wrote nothing and exactly one post-commit row exists.

**The live apply's own throws emit too (R8 F1).** `applyStagedCore` contains no catch; a throw from `runPhase2` or the in-tx floor/audit/pending-row operations (`lib/sync/applyStagedCore.ts:600-661`) would escape `applyLiveWithDriveReverify` and bypass the post-resolve applied emit entirely — an attempt that did real sync work, terminating with no row. `applyStaged`'s live branch therefore wraps the `applyLiveWithDriveReverify` await: on catch, emit `{ driveFileId, outcome: "parse_error", code: classifySyncFailure(error), payload: errorPayload(error) }` through the same resolved sink (NULL duration, escaped-throw rule §1.1) and rethrow the original error — the routes' existing typed-500 mappings are unchanged. No tracker is needed on this surface: the staged path installs no sink into the core (the tail is stripped sinkless, above), so at catch time this surface has written zero family-(i) rows by construction; a sink failure inside the catch escalates under `SYNC_LOG_EMIT_FAILED`, original error still thrown.

**Throw-surface closure table.** This was the fourth thrown-runner surface found one at a time; the class is now closed by enumeration over §3's surfaces, and the table IS the review fence — a fifth entry means a new §3 surface was added, not that this list leaks:

### 3.5 Shared-branch sweep: dark terminal branches in the shared pipeline (R1 F1, R4 F3)

Reviewing the claim "every non-applied `processOneFile_unlocked` outcome emits" refuted it — twice. R1 found two dark branches; R4 found two more inside a function R1's sweep had already visited. Per the class-sweep rule, the cover is now a DERIVATION, not a branch list: **the unit of proof is `handleFetchFailure_unlocked`'s input partition as modeled today** — `code` class (`STAGED_PARSE_SOURCE_GONE` / `PARSE_ERROR_LAST_GOOD` / generic `SyncFailureCode`) × `show` row (present / absent) × `existingPending` (present / absent) — and the §5 matrix test asserts every cell of that partition writes exactly one row. **This is a regression pin over the modeled partition, not an exhaustiveness proof (R5 F2):** the function branches on data, so no `never`-check exists to force a future arm into the matrix; an arm keyed on a predicate outside the modeled partition (a specific new failure code, a new `fileMeta`/`binding` condition) is the filed derived-guard class (`BL-SYNC-LOG-ATTRIBUTION-METATEST`, §6), same as every other writer-reachability gap. What the matrix DOES buy: any change to an existing cell's emission fails it, and a new arm that subdivides a modeled value is caught when its cell's fixture selects it.

**Row shape on the already-emitting arms is ratified, not corrected (R5 F1).** The existing recovery emissions record `outcome: "error"` while returning `source_gone`/`parse_error` (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2713-2715`, `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2749-2751`) — the same convention as the manual recovery sinks (`lib/sync/runManualSyncForShow.ts:172-177`). Rewriting those rows' outcome would change what established cron/manual consumers read, for zero diagnostic gain (the `status` column is `code ?? outcome` either way, `lib/sync/syncLog.ts:17-19`). The returned-terminal-VALUE rule — record what the arm returns, never its input — therefore binds the **code** on the arms this arc repairs (the carve records `SYNC_FILE_FAILED`); the pre-existing arms keep their recovery-row shape and the matrix pins them as they are.

The repairs, all in the same PR (one-line emissions, no product decision):

- **`handleFetchFailure_unlocked`'s existing-pending early return** (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2692-2696`): a fetch failure while a live pending review exists returns `source_gone`/`parse_error` with **no** `insertSyncLog`, while every sibling branch of the same function writes one (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2647-2652`, `lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2713-2719`). Repair: bind the branch's return value first, then record THAT value — `const terminal = code === "PARSE_ERROR_LAST_GOOD" && !show ? { outcome: "parse_error", code: SYNC_FILE_FAILED } : result;` followed by `recoveryTx.insertSyncLog({ driveFileId, outcome: terminal.outcome, code: terminal.code, payload: { driveFileId, message: errorMessage(error), existing_pending: true } })` and `return terminal`. Recording `result` directly would log `PARSE_ERROR_LAST_GOOD` on the first-seen carve whose actual terminal code is `SYNC_FILE_FAILED` (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2693-2695`) — the row must state what the branch returned, never its input (R2 F1). NULL duration (recovery sink carries no start — parent spec §3.3.1 row 3, unchanged posture).
- **Both no-show fetch-failure arms** (R4 F3): the first-seen `PARSE_ERROR_LAST_GOOD` carve (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2805-2819`) and the generic no-show arm (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:2821-2830`) each upsert a `pending_ingestions` row and return a terminal `parse_error`/`source_gone` with no `sync_log` row. Repair: each arm writes `recoveryTx.insertSyncLog({ driveFileId, outcome: <terminal>.outcome, code: <terminal>.code, payload: { driveFileId, message: errorMessage(error) } })` for the value it RETURNS (the carve records `SYNC_FILE_FAILED`, same terminal-value rule as the existing-pending return). NULL duration, tx-bound like the sibling recovery writes.
- **The pull-sheet-override TOCTOU skip** (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3514-3518`): the under-lock snapshot comparison returns `{ outcome: "skipped", reason: "pull_sheet_override_changed_under_lock" }` with no row, while every adjacent skip branch of `processOneFile_unlocked` emits. Repair: `await logSync(txDeps, driveFileId, result)` before the return — same in-lock shape as its neighbors (`lib/sync/runScheduledCronSync.ts:3480-3495`), duration via the threaded start.

All repairs benefit cron identically (the branches are shared); that is in-scope class-sweep of this arc's defect shape — "a terminal outcome with no row" — not new scope.

## 6. Documented limits

Deliberate, and not findings:

- **Non-applied live staged-apply outcomes write no `sync_log` row** (§3.4 rationale). If operational need emerges, that is a new scope decision, not a regression of this one.
- **Wizard staged applies and all §1.1 ratified-silent refusal branches stay silent.**
- **Injected-sink (family i) emissions can record an attempt whose enclosing tx later rolls back** (the sink's connection commits independently) — at worst one over-reported aborted attempt, never an under-reported completed one. Recovery-sink (family ii) writes have the mirror-image exposure: they roll back WITH an aborted tx and vanish — which the tracked catches compensate for by emitting a durable `parse_error` when no family-(i) row exists (§3.1/§3.3). Both exposures are the established topology (§1.1), not new to this arc.
- **`duration_ms` on the staged-apply row measures from `applyStaged` entry** — route-level auth/body parsing excluded; the parent §3.3 runner-boundary limit, on a third surface.
- **Thrown-attempt rows carry NULL duration** (§1.1, parent §3.3.1).
- **The retry route's outer catch (`PENDING_INGESTION_RETRY_FAILED`) does not additionally write `sync_log`.** With the §3.1 prepare guard AND the `_unlocked` runner's tracked catch in place, the throws that reach it without a row are pre-attempt route work — auth, the id-read, the row-lock read, the Drive metadata fetch (which already returns a typed 502 with its own breadcrumb, `app/api/admin/pending-ingestions/[id]/retry/route.ts:416-430`) — and those remain `app_events`-only, as today.
- **A future caller that bypasses a covered chokepoint gets no row and no missing-sink signal.** Two shapes: a manual-runner caller omitting the optional sink, and a direct `applyStaged_unlocked` caller skipping the locked wrapper's post-commit region (today that export has no production caller outside the wrapper's own lock closures, `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1874`, `lib/sync/applyStaged.ts:1922`).
