**Judgment:** Two patterns are worth naming, and the second is the one I would
tell another arc about.

The first is the coverage-claim class, which took three rounds to close. A task
marker claimed four acceptance criteria whose cases did not exist. Round 1
caught it, round 2 found the repair half-done, round 3 found the demoted-seed
route still indistinguishable from the panel-refetch route because a stub fired
during render and a single-valued fetch let either path produce the asserted
number. Each round the claim narrowed toward what the tests actually
discriminated. The repair that finally held was not another case but a fixture
change: two routes, two different numbers, so only one path can produce the
assertion, probed in both directions.

The second is recogniser widening, and this arc is a clean specimen. The AC-20
transition audit and the live-region scanners took exactly one new input family
per round: a line-regex became an AST walk (R2), counts became identities and
the template-literal spelling was added (R3), and R4 arrived with nested guards
and JSX spread attributes. Four rounds, four widenings, finding rate flat. That
is the treadmill `AGENTS.md` names, and the prescribed repair is narrowing, not
another parser pass.

So round 4's repair is deliberately asymmetric, and the asymmetry is the
content. Repo-wide, the scanners DECLINE to fire on a JSX spread and the limit
is written into the source: firing was tried and produced 35 hits across the
live corpus, nearly all ordinary elements that merely carry a spread, and a
guard that reds on correct code gets exempted into uselessness. On the
nav-scoped surface, six files this arc owns with no spread in them today, the
spread IS refused, because there an unclassifiable element is worth failing on.
And AC-20 stopped claiming a "fail-by-default inventory": it pins the SET of
conditions present, not reachability or nesting, and says so, with the
nested-guard mutant left to the behavioural terminality cases that already catch
it. Narrowing the claim was cheaper than a fourth widening and is the only move
that ends the sequence.

The cost of learning this late is measurable: three of the four rounds' findings
were on the guard surfaces rather than on the feature.
