**Judgment:** rounds 1-3 are one vector, and the round-3 repair is the one that mattered — not because it found more escapes, but because it stopped enumerating them. Every earlier version asked "which unknown labels exist?", which ranges over an open set, so each round escaped whatever bound the previous repair had introduced. Replacing it with "is any separator left unconsumed?" moved the question to the closed set the accept-set itself defines, and the seven R2 escapes became one answer rather than seven patches. R3's residual then had no structural signal at all to key on, which is what made it a limit rather than a defect: probed character by character, every cited input still renders in full — the imperfection is which ROW the text lands in, not whether it survives.

R4 is the most valuable round in this segment and it is a finding against the tests, not the product. The limit's evidence and its guard had the same weakness: a per-word `toContain` loop proves presence and nothing about order, duplication, or whitespace. It is the containment-is-not-verbatim trap, and it appeared in the very test written to substantiate a no-rewrite claim. Repaired to assert each line's non-label content survives as one contiguous run, mutant-verified against the reviewer's own reordering probe and against a character-drop.

R5 is the same lesson as R4 arriving through the product instead of the tests: a repair that fixes ONE field of a shape leaves its siblings, and the siblings escaped four further rounds because the guard's own test only exercised the field that had been fixed. The repair moves the check to the acceptance gate — one sentinel anywhere returns the whole cell to the raw row — and the new sweep crosses every sentinel with every derived field rather than sampling one cell of that matrix.

R6 is the third distinct way one test claimed a mechanism it did not exercise, and the three are worth naming together because each survived a repair of the others: a fixture whose ids correlated with its names (so id-sorting passed), then a fixture whose winner was already first (so not sorting at all passed), and separately a preservation loop that proved presence rather than order. The pattern is a guard whose input cannot distinguish the mechanism from its accidents; the repair each time was to make the input DISAGREE along every axis the mechanism could be confused with, and to state that disagreement as a premise rather than leave it to the reader.

**Mechanizable:** none filed, and deliberately. The candidate — "ban per-word `toContain` loops over an input's tokens" — would fire on legitimate uses, and the real rule it points at (containment is not verbatim) is already written in the guard-design memory. What WOULD have prevented rounds 1-3 is likewise already written: the repair-economy rule says a recognizer bounded by a number will be found by the next reviewer and to derive the bound from the input instead. That rule existed and was not applied until round 3. The gap was adherence, not tooling, so a new gate would add noise without adding a signal the corpus lacks.
