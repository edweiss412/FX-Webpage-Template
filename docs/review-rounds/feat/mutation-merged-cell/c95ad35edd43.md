# Review-round economy — `feat/mutation-merged-cell` @ `c95ad35edd43`

## diff — 3 rounds

**Examined:** every `diff` dispatch on this arc. FIVE dispatches, but the corpus counts
THREE rounds, and the gap is worth stating: one whole-diff dispatch returned `no_verdict`
on an attempt timeout and is not counted at all, and the two scoped reviews each number
their own rounds from 1, so review B's round 1 collides with review A's. Counted rounds are
distinct `round` VALUES on verdict-bearing rows, not dispatches.

Eight findings across the five dispatches: A r1 = 2 P1, A r2 = 2 P1, A r3 = 1 P1,
B r1 = 3. Every one was accepted; none was refuted, relitigated, or reversed.

**Mechanizable:** three of the eight, and they share one cause worth naming: **the last two
rounds were spent on MY optimization of the row scanner, not on the detector.** The product
behavior — modal width, the tie-guard, the row floor, detection-not-correction — has been
stable since round 2. What kept failing was an allocation-free index scan I wrote to make
cell counting cheap:

- A r2: the scan exempted the first cell from validation (`cells > 0`, where
  `cells` increments after the check).
- A r3: the scan checked character membership rather than the `:?-+:?` ORDER, so
  `--:--`, `::---` and `- - -` passed as delimiter cells.

Both split sections that should not have split, and both are the same defect class: a
hand-rolled scanner replacing a regex that was already correct.

The mechanization is not another guard. It is a **measurement that should have preceded the
optimization**, and it settles the question outright: over the 17-fixture corpus the clever
scan costs 71ms and the obvious split-and-regex costs 578ms, against **16,868ms for
`parseSheet` itself** — 0.4% of a parse versus 3.4%. The justification I wrote for the
optimization ("3x slower on the typo-generator suites") was false; those suites are slow
under machine load and fail identically on `main`, where this detector does not exist. The
scanner is now the obvious implementation, which removes the defect class rather than
guarding it.

**Judgment:** the two round-1 findings were genuine design gaps and neither was
mechanizable in advance — escaped in-cell pipes (`\|`) inflating the column count, and a
section boundary derived from a label registry instead of markdown's own structure. The
second went through two wrong answers before the right one: first a routing-table lookup,
then a header-registry lookup, and only then the observation that an **alignment row** is
the structural evidence and no registry is needed at all. The lesson is the one the
class-sweep rule already states — sweep to a derivation, not a longer list — and I applied
it one level too shallow twice before reaching it.

**Infra:** the first whole-diff dispatch died at the 20-minute attempt timeout because the
reviewer re-ran the mutation harness (~35 minutes) as part of its investigation. Split
tight-scope briefs plus an explicit "do not run the harness, here are its numbers" budget
line fixed it; every later dispatch returned a verdict. Separately, the machine carried
about six concurrent agent sessions at load average 44-50 throughout, which cost two full
harness runs to a 1-hour per-shard hook timeout and made several suites fail on time rather
than on assertions — each confirmed against `main` before being set aside.
