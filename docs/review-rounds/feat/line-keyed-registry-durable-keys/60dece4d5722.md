**Mechanizable:** The dominant class, by a wide margin, was **a number in prose that no command
produced**. It accounted for 5 of 5 in R1, 5 of 5 in R2, and 7 of 7 in R3 — seventeen of
seventeen findings across three rounds, all one shape.

The instructive part is that the document STATED the rule against this in R1 ("§4 states no
anchorability figure that the committed census does not print") and then broke it seventeen
times. Stating a rule is not enforcing one. Three things would have closed it earlier, in
increasing order of value:

1. **Run the sweep the rule implies, at authoring time.** Extract every numeric literal, diff it
   against what the committed commands print, and account for each survivor. Done at R3 it took
   minutes and found the same class the reviewer was finding one round at a time.
2. **Emit derived quantities from the tool, not from arithmetic in prose.** `--derivability`,

   in a paragraph; each was wrong at least once before it was printed.
3. **Subtract.** The finding rate did not decay across three rounds (5, 5, 7) because the defect

**Judgment:** Two findings turned on questions no lint could settle, and both were resolved by
measuring rather than arguing.

- **R1 finding 4** established that 27 of 47 rows on the candidate registry carry hand-authored
  anchor fields, so the "content anchor" would have compared registry fields to registry fields.
  The arc's own prototype passed 14/14 and did not catch it, because it compared registry-parsed
  anchors against registry-parsed anchors and never extracted from a live site. **A green that
  never touches the real input is a green about nothing**, and that is the transferable lesson.
- **R3 finding 3** caught R2's repair being backwards: 8-vs-9 is a policy difference (whole-group
  refusal versus per-row resolution), not a grouping difference. A repair that introduces the
  next round's defect is the fix-round regression shape, and the cost of getting a reconciliation
