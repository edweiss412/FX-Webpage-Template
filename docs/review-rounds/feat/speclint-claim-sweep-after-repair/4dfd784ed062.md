**Seven rounds of a recognizer that never grew is the result worth recording.** The standing rule under
same-axis recurrence is that the repair direction is narrowing, and this stage is what that looks like
when it is followed from round 1: the arm ends with FOUR codes, a closed input set, and TEN declared
limits, and every round moved a claim out of the mechanism and into either a declaration or §5. The
count is high and the artifact is smaller than it was at round 1. What the count measures here is a
design being narrowed to what it can prove, not a reviewer's imagination.

**Round 6's findings were consistency defects and round 7's was the repair's own.** Round 6: §3.4 said
"two codes" while normatively enumerating three, and §3.1 cited `§5 item 6` for a limit that is item 7 —
the second had a TWIN in the probe record (§5.1, same stale reference) found by the class sweep rather
than by the reviewer, one finding, two instances, both repaired in one commit. Its HIGH was a precision
fix rather than a mechanism: `(code, doc, line, token)` cannot express two survivors on one line, which
the live corpus has eight times over.

**Round 7 was a BOUNDED CONFIRMATION round against a closed six-repair surface, and it earned its
place by finding the one thing such a round is for: a defect the REPAIR introduced.** Closing the output
set at three codes collided with a requirement §3.2 had carried since exact matching landed — a declared
identifier occurring zero times must be reported NOT FOUND, and none of the three codes could carry it
without inventing a location. The repair is not "add a fourth code": §3.4 now DERIVES the inventory,
tabling every normative outcome in §3 against one of three channels (FINDING, REFUSAL, DECLARED
SILENCE), because a set closed by enumeration re-opens the moment a requirement is written in one
section with no channel in another — which is exactly what had happened. The §3.0 refusals are stated as
adapter-level usage errors that exit 2 and emit no finding, so a swept-and-clean run can never be
confused with a run that never started. **A closure that cannot be re-opened by enumeration is what the
seventh round bought**, and it is the same repair direction as the other six: less mechanism, more
declaration.

**The recurrence this arc paid for, and it is the arc's own subject.** Three number-drift repairs
(936 → 943 → 947 → 953) were each chased as corpus growth when the growth was this arc's own writing —
its spec and probe record are documents in the corpus its arm measures. The fourth instance landed
INSIDE the section written to declare the hazard. **That is the stale-predecessor class this arm exists
to catch, committed four times by the author specifying it.** The repair was structural rather than a
fresher number: one population declared by path, one script emitting every §2 row from it, and no
arc-inclusive figure stated anywhere in either document — because writing one moves it.

**Mechanizable:** declined: two of the 24 are mechanizable and BOTH WERE BUILT IN-BRANCH, so neither
warrants a ledger row — the mechanism ships with the arc rather than waiting on one.

  mechanical form is `probes/scripts/2026-08-20-population-census.py`: `ARC_DOCUMENTS` names this arc's

- **The claimed-repair sweep.** `probes/scripts/2026-08-20-claimed-repair-sweep.py` verifies BOTH directions: every repair claimed in

  survivor was a defect in the WITNESS, not the document — the historical `947 of 947` figure the repair

**Twelve tasks became eleven became NINE, and every repair was structural rather than a better `why=`.**

**The other eleven were proof-shaped defects of a different kind, and two are worth naming.** Round 3
