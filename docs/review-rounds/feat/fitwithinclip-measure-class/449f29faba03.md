**Rounds 3 and 4 are one story, and it is the expensive one: §0.1 was rewritten three times because the spec kept MODELLING the consumers instead of measuring them.** Draft one measured the unit harness. Round 3 established that Strict Mode is on and replays a cleanup-returning ref, and that no consumer uses the unit harness's shape — so draft two re-measured on a conditional-host probe and generalised it to "all five call sites". Round 4 killed that too, with the sharpest finding of the arc: there are **three** lifecycles, not one. `ReSyncButton` is a bare conditional host; `PublishedToggle`'s `reapplyKey` IS the condition that mounts the node, so both change in one commit; and `AttentionMenu` calls the hook inside `AttentionMenuPanel`, where the node is present at that owner's first render — so the always-present shape draft two dismissed as used by no route **is** a shipped surface.

Draft three enumerates all three from their component source, measures each in both modes, and pins each with its own case. It also corrected the arc's story in both directions: `BL-FITWITHINCLIP-DOUBLE-MOUNT-MEASURE`'s premise holds for two consumers of three rather than "every mount" or "none", and the development regression the spec had generalised to every appearance turns out to be one cell of twelve.

That class — **a measurement taken on a fixture whose shape differs from the production call sites** — is not mechanizable. Nothing can tell a harness from a route without knowing what the routes render. It is exactly what the empirical-spike rule exists for, and the lesson is narrower than that rule currently states: probing before drafting is not enough if the probe models one caller. **Enumerate the call sites from source FIRST, then probe each.** Three rounds bought that sentence; it is written here so the next arc buys it for nothing.

**Round 5 charged the same §0.1 defect a fourth time, in its last remaining form: the table's LABEL.** The numbers were right; "per overlay appearance" was not. The table measures the ATTACH, which for `ReSyncButton` and `PublishedToggle` is the whole appearance and for `AttentionMenu` is not — its `reapplyKey` is an entrance flag flipped from a mount-scoped `requestAnimationFrame`, so one menu opening is an attach AND a key change AND a re-attach. A case asserting the attach row while driving that flip is unsatisfiable unless the re-attach is suppressed, and suppressing it would leave the `scale-95` entrance's transformed geometry stale without tripping the floor-clamp diagnostic — silently wrong, which the bound forbids. §0.1a now carries the settled lifecycle separately (bare 3/3/6 to 2/2/2, Strict 6/4/8 to 4/3/3) and (h17) asserts both snapshots rather than one total. Round 5 also found two prose sentences still claiming no consumer attaches at its owner's first render, which §0.1 had already corrected two rounds earlier — the stale-prose class, again.

**Four rounds on one section is the arc's real cost, and the lesson is narrow enough to be reusable.** Draft one measured the wrong fixture; draft two measured one caller and generalised; draft three measured all three callers but mislabelled what the measurement covered. Each was a smaller error than the last, which is convergence, but the class was constant: **a measurement is not finished until its DOMAIN and its BOUNDARY are both written down** — which callers it ranges over, and where in the lifecycle it starts and stops. The empirical-spike rule already says to probe before drafting. It does not say to enumerate the callers from source first and to state what the probe's window covers. Those two sentences are what four rounds bought.

**Round 6 found the arc's cheapest-to-miss defect and confirmed the expensive section is finished.** It verified §0.1 and §0.1a independently — "reproduced every count exactly" — and confirmed the committed callable-ref probe covered all 902 applicable source files and all 120 `ref` attributes with no pre-count omissions. The class is **a mandated inventory whose rows cite a pin that does not exercise them.** The transition-audit rule requires the table; nothing required each row's citation to be checked against what the cited case actually does. **Round 7 found the round-6 repair still wrong, and for a better reason than round 6's.** Round 6 said §3.1's `N → F` row cited the wrong case. Round 7 said there is no `N → F` edge reachable the way the spec imagined at all: with a stable ref and an unchanged `reapplyKey`, **a re-render does nothing** — not even when the DOM's clip status changes in that same commit. Probed:

```
stable-key rerender clips=false→true  cap=""       applies=0
resize + frame                        cap="322px"  applies=1
```

So `F ↔ N` is driven only by a re-measure SIGNAL, and a `reapplyKey` change is `X → D → Y` rather than a direct edge. That made family A `F → D → N` and family B `F → D → F`, not the direct rows the table claimed — and the proposed (h19) would have FAILED the correct implementation, because it asserted a cap appearing on a re-render.

**Three rounds on §3.1 is the second same-vector train of this arc, and the diagnosis is the same shape as the first.** §0.1 took four rounds because it kept modelling the callers instead of measuring them. §3.1 took three because it kept modelling the state machine instead of probing what causes each edge. Both were prose reasoning about a runtime the probe could have answered in a minute. The general rule, and it is not currently written anywhere: **a state-machine inventory is a claim about MECHANISM, not just about coverage — for each edge, probe what causes it before writing the row.** A row that names the right pair of states and the wrong cause reads exactly like a correct row, and its cited test passes.
