That class — **a measurement taken on a fixture whose shape differs from the production call sites** — is not mechanizable. Nothing can tell a harness from a route without knowing what the routes render. It is exactly what the empirical-spike rule exists for, and the lesson is narrower than that rule currently states: probing before drafting is not enough if the probe models one caller. **Enumerate the call sites from source FIRST, then probe each.** Three rounds bought that sentence; it is written here so the next arc buys it for nothing.

**Round 5 charged the same §0.1 defect a fourth time, in its last remaining form: the table's LABEL.** The numbers were right; "per overlay appearance" was not. The table measures the ATTACH, which for `ReSyncButton` and `PublishedToggle` is the whole appearance and for `AttentionMenu` is not — its `reapplyKey` is an entrance flag flipped from a mount-scoped `requestAnimationFrame`, so one menu opening is an attach AND a key change AND a re-attach. Round 5 also found two prose sentences still claiming no consumer attaches at its owner's first render, which §0.1 had already corrected two rounds earlier — the stale-prose class, again.

**Four rounds on one section is the arc's real cost, and the lesson is narrow enough to be reusable.** Draft one measured the wrong fixture; draft two measured one caller and generalised; draft three measured all three callers but mislabelled what the measurement covered. Each was a smaller error than the last, which is convergence, but the class was constant: **a measurement is not finished until its DOMAIN and its BOUNDARY are both written down** — which callers it ranges over, and where in the lifecycle it starts and stops. The empirical-spike rule already says to probe before drafting. It does not say to enumerate the callers from source first and to state what the probe's window covers. Those two sentences are what four rounds bought.

Round 4 also caught the browser sampler's premise asserting only that sampling happened, not that the recorder was ARMED before the overlay appeared — which would have made the new case a slower copy of the two after-settle cases it was meant to complement. Rendering an existing prose contract executable, rather than restating it, is the repair.
