**Judgment:** the round-count driver was a single axis, and naming it is the lesson. The arc's rounds 2, 4 and 5 (this file's R2 and R3, plus one in the sibling) were all one shape — **a success value asserted as a literal beside the check that was supposed to
establish it** — at three different sites in the unenrollable runner: the kill fold, the exit-status
assignment, the baseline call. Each was individually real and individually severe (each produced a
19/19 perfect score from a harness that had run almost nothing), and each was repaired correctly.
But the first two were repaired AS INSTANCES, which is what bought rounds 4 and 5.

The repair that ended it was structural rather than additive, per AGENTS.md's repair direction:
`assertBrowserBaseline` returns its own proof and the caller binds it, so the value cannot be
obtained without running the check and deleting the check leaves it unbound. Three of these could
have been one round had the class been named after round 2 — the tell was available then, since the
round-2 repair had already MOVED a flip site into an enrolled module rather than guarding it.

The arc's R1 was a sequencing error, not a defect: the ledger rows were still `IN PROGRESS` because the
graduation commit had not yet landed, and AC-7 requires it to precede the final review. Its R3 was a
guard that could be satisfied by its own prose (`/byte-identical|\.equals\(/`, an alternation the
test TITLE matched) — the anti-tautology trap, in a guard written to close a different one.
