Two mechanizable rules come out of it, both cheap and both already applied here:

1. **A recognizer bounded by a NUMBER will be found by the next reviewer.** Rounds 1-5 of the emphasis matcher widened a cap from one delimiter to two to three; round 2 removed the cap entirely and round 4 still found a shape — because the second cap was positional, not numeric. The repair that closes a class states the closed set (four positions in `LABEL : VALUE`), never a bound.
2. **A key that can ALIAS is not a clock.** The count-recency rule cost three rounds keyed on the wrong thing (the attempt, then the session) before landing on a monotonic per-attempt ordinal. "Is there another way two turns share a session" terminates; "is there another aliasing case" does not.

The `Number()` pair is the plainest mechanizable miss: round 3's finding and round 4's were the *same defect in two call sites*, and the round-3 repair swept only one. That is the class-sweep rule in AGENTS.md failing in exactly the way it warns about, on the arc that cites it.

**Judgment:** Whether to keep repairing at all past round 2. Each round's findings were genuinely in the losing direction — a lost verdict files a completed review as an infrastructure fault, and a restated count puts a number in the corpus the reviewer never wrote — so the consequence bound admitted every one of them, and none was a "the guard does not pin what it claims" ratchet. Continuing was right, but only because the bound held; the moment findings had turned into "here is one more shape that leaks", `BL-CODEX-GUARD-COMMONMARK-PARSE` was already filed to take them.

Also judgment: a mutation probe, not the reviewer, found the round-3 repair's own gap (writer 4 never placed its attempt on the clock). Probing each repair with the mutant that should kill it caught a defect four review rounds had not, at a fraction of a round's cost. Worth doing before the dispatch, not after.

**Infra:** None. No round was lost to a reaped dispatch, a wedged app-server, or a `no_verdict` result; all four returned a declared verdict and a declared `FINDINGS:` count on the first attempt.
