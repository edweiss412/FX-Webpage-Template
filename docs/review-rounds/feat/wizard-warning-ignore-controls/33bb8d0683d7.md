**Judgment:** the round's findings were one shape wearing three costumes, and the shape
is *a rule enforced at one call site instead of at its source*.

The rail/panel disagreement is the clearest case. `wizardPanelCount` was written so the
heading and the rail would share one predicate, and its own comment says a rail and a
heading that disagree is the defect it exists to prevent. It shipped anyway, because
the discipline was applied to the function while its INPUTS were computed
independently — the panel reconciled indices inside a local closure, the rail counted
the raw partition. A shared helper fed two different numbers is not a shared predicate.

The both-store ignore is the same shape at the data layer: the model was asked to name
one store when the honest answer was two, and the previous round had already "fixed"
this once by choosing the other horn. That earlier fix was sound reasoning applied
one-sidedly, which is what made it survive review — it explains correctly why the
durable route is wrong and never asks whether its own route has the same flaw.

The Flow-B carry is the shape at the persistence layer, and it is the one review would
have been least likely to catch by reading: the field was added to the shadow payload
correctly, consumed correctly by finalize-cas, and simply not mentioned by a third
consumer that rebuilds state from the same payload. Nothing about the diff points at
that file; it is reachable only by asking who else reads this carrier.

R2 found one more instance of the very same shape, which is the strongest evidence the
diagnosis is right: `target` decided the ignore backend AND was read for the report's
show identity, so rerouting the ignore to staging filed the report against nothing. One
value answering two questions is the same defect as one rule enforced at one caller.

R3 is the round worth being honest about: both its findings were regressions from R1's
own repairs, and both were P0. The Flow-B carry was placed at the restage site, so the
`hard_failed` exit deleted the shadow and returned before reaching it — the repair
addressed the path in view rather than the hazard, which is the DELETE. And the new
`both` origin silently inherited a fail-open: the durable loader degrades an
`infra_error` to an empty set, correct for rendering, and once that value routed a
mutation an unresolved read became indistinguishable from "not durable".

The pattern across both: a repair that adds a decision inherits every existing
degradation feeding it, and a repair placed at the site you were reading covers that
site rather than the class. Neither was caught by the round that introduced it, and
both were mechanically findable after the fact — the first by enumerating exits between
the shadow read and the restore, the second by asking which consumers of a fail-open
value route a mutation rather than a render. Those two derivations are now written into
the repairs' own comments so the next reader inherits the question, not just the answer.

**Mechanizable:** none new, and one worth naming as NOT mechanizable. The class sweeps
that made this round's repairs correct were both derivations — "every consumer of the
shadow payload, against every field it carries" and "every site that counts or indexes
the partition" — and both were answered by grep in under a minute. What no lint could
have supplied is the judgment that the *second* dropped shadow field belonged in the
same commit: `useRawDecisions` is another arc's feature, and the rule that put it in
this PR is the class-sweep disposition default, not a detector.
