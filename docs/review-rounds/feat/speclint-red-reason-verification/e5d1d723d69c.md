## spec — 4 rounds

**Examined:** all four spec rounds (R1 NEEDS-ATTENTION 3, R2 BLOCKING 4, R3 BLOCKING 2, R4 BLOCKING 3)
on `docs/superpowers/specs/ci/2026-08-21-speclint-red-reason-verification-design.md`, their briefs,
the repair commits `30ecf0ead`, `65e11414d`, `9ec06fada`, `a89cf7639`, `0925d64ec`, and the two
committed probes `probe/population.mts` and `probe/reach.mts`.

**Judgment:** rounds 1 and 2 did the work adversarial review exists for, and round 2 paid for the arc
by itself. Its blocking finding refuted the deliverable's central premise using this repo's OWN prior
measurements, one spec over: a module-scope `premise()` throws during collection, so vitest reports
zero cases while an assertion ran and failed. That retired the observable rather than repairing it.
Three of round 2's four findings were a single class, a criterion that does not exercise the thing it
names, and retiring the observable removed the class instead of patching three instances.

**The signal that ended this arc was subject classification, not rate.** The finding count did not
decay (3, 4, 2, 3). What changed is what the findings were AGAINST: three consecutive rounds found
defects in the spec's DESCRIPTION of a repair whose MECHANISM had been stable since round 2, and
round 4 explicitly confirmed all four design claims it was pointed at. Classify what each finding is
against, rather than waiting for the count to reach zero.

The deliverable SHRANK at every round. An arm plus a repair at R1, a repair alone at R2, a
correctly-sized repair at R3, an honest description of it at R4. That is the opposite of the ratchet
the round cap defends against, and it is why four rounds here were not four rounds of the failure
mode. No finding in any round was refuted; twelve of twelve were accepted.

**Mechanizable:** two shapes, one of them the arc's signature.

The first is a reporting discipline, not a guard. Four times a count was reported from a SUBSET of an
instrument's output rather than the total the instrument printed: the reach went 25, then 10, then 9,
and is 15. After the first, the instrument was correct every time. `probe/population.mts` printed
`none: 15` in its derivation totals two lines above the `pnpm heavy`-wrapped block that was read
instead, and round 3's blocking finding was that misreading reaching the spec. The rule the
orchestrator banked from it: quote the instrument's printed line verbatim in specs and reports, never
a number recalled from it. A number that has been retyped has left the instrument's custody. Not
filed as a ledger row, because it is an authoring discipline rather than a missing arm, and the mint
bar's incident requirement is discharged by this filing.

The second is already a known class in this repo and was committed anyway. Round 4's blocking finding
was that `probe/reach.mts`, added at the round cap specifically to make the criterion mechanical,
PRINTED its per-line results and asserted nothing, and reconciled `silent + carried === 15` — a check
that can never fail, because every inspected line increments exactly one side. The repair holds both
named sets (fifteen v2, sixteen v1), asserts per-line expectations in two modes, and fails on drift;
three mutations demonstrate it can fail. A guard that only ever passes is the shape this repo has
paid for before, and writing one into the very artifact meant to end enumeration is worth recording
plainly.

A third, cheaper miss: `pnpm typecheck` was red on this branch from round 2 onward because a probe
imported with `.ts` extensions (TS5097). `tsx` resolved them, so every local run passed and only the
compiler could see it. No review round caught it and none could have, since it is not visible in the
document under review. The existing pre-push discipline covers it; the miss was skipping the check.

declined: neither shape is schedulable work. The first is an authoring discipline with no detector
worth writing: "the number in this prose came from a different line of the tool's output than the one
it names" is not decidable from the document, and the mechanized form already exists as the rule that
the instrument's printed line is quoted verbatim. The second is a known class this repo has already
mechanized where it can be — `pnpm mutation:guards` exists precisely to find assertions that cannot
fail — and this probe is not registry-expressible, being a corpus-walking script rather than a module
with a referring suite. The honest repair was to mutate it by hand, which this arc did three times.
Filing either as a ledger row would restate an existing rule rather than add enforcement, which the
2026-08-04 filing bar screens out.

**Infra:** all four rounds ran in a sandbox that denied the `tsx` IPC socket and Vite's temp
directory. Every reviewer said so explicitly rather than reporting a clean run, and `node --import
tsx` worked as the substitute. Recorded because a verdict token does not carry its own scope: these
were static-plus-read-only-probe verdicts, not executable verification. Round 4 also found that the
oracle's own child process still shelled out through `pnpm exec tsx`, recreating inside the probe the
exact denial the outer invocation had been documented to avoid.

**What the cap actually produced.** The convergence gate blocked at round 4 and offered its three
options; the arc took option 3, replacing enumeration with a mechanical oracle. That oracle settled
the counts terminally and surfaced a design decision three rounds of review had not reached: three of
the fifteen lines already carry a hard finding, one of them the live marker sitting exactly on the
hazard `synthesizeCollectionFindings` warns about in its own comment. Round 4 was then spent
deliberately as a confirming round on the one thing an oracle cannot ratify, the judgment that
decision embodies, and it found the oracle itself unsound. Both halves of that are the intended
behaviour of the cap: it forced the mechanism, and the mechanism got reviewed.

## plan — 4 rounds

**Examined:** all four plan rounds (R1 BLOCKING 3, R2 BLOCKING 2, R3 BLOCKING 2, R4 BLOCKING 2) on
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-21-speclint-red-reason-verification.md`, their briefs, and the repair
commits `8afd19ba6`, `6a5b2701c`, `f9e6719d4`.

**Judgment:** round 1 found a genuine structural defect and the rest found bookkeeping. R1's blocking
finding was that the two-task split violated invariant 1: Task 1 implemented the complete repair, so
Task 2's partition assertion was GREEN the moment it was authored, which is a task whose acceptance
condition is that it cannot fail. The plan's own §4 had already written the rule against that split.
Collapsing to one task with five assertions closed it, and rounds 2, 3 and 4 each confirmed the five
rows discriminate as claimed, so the design has been settled since round 1.

The two findings worth the rounds they cost were both about the gap between what the plan CLAIMED and
what it would actually DO. R1 found `probe/reach.mts` named as the closed criterion and scheduled
nowhere. R3 found that scheduling it before the commit was not enough, because `lint-staged` rewrites
the tree on every commit, so no pre-commit check describes the committed tree. R4 then found the
correction still under-scoped: the plan asserted line 717 was above the edit and unaffected, and it is
not, because the union-member additions land at lines 605 and 610 and shift every cited line below
them. Each of those three is the same question asked one layer deeper: does the thing the document
promises actually happen to the tree that lands.

**Mechanizable:** one shape, found four times, and the matcher widened at every one.

Round 1's task collapse invalidated counts throughout the document. R2 found six stale plurality
claims; the repair swept plurality WORDS and reported the class closed. R3 found three more written as
universal quantifiers, and the author independently found two more written as spelled numbers, in the
gap R3's own brief had predicted. R4 found two survivors still.

The rule the arc paid four rounds for: **a sweep is only as wide as its matcher, and the matcher has
to derive from the defect's DOMAIN rather than from the instances that were reported.** For a defect
about counts that domain is numerals, spelled numbers, plurality words and universal quantifiers. The
reported instances are a sample of the domain, not its boundary, so a class-closed report whose
matcher is undeclared is a claim about the matcher rather than about the class.

declined: not schedulable as a detector. A count claim's truth depends on what it counts, which is
prose semantics, not structure; `spec:lint`'s `NUMERIC_NOUN_MISMATCH` and `SIBLING_LIST_CARDINALITY`
already mechanize the two decidable corners of it, and both fired usefully on this document during the
arc. The residue is an authoring discipline, and filing a row asking for a detector that reads intent
would be an entry nobody can act on, which the 2026-08-04 filing bar screens out.

**Infra:** every plan round hit the same sandbox limit as the spec rounds and every one said so:
Vitest could not run, failing before collection with `EPERM` creating Vite's temporary `ssr`
directory. So no round verified the plan's reds by execution, and all four verdicts are static plus
read-only probing. The probes that COULD run were run in every round, and R4's line-shift measurement
was itself produced by a read-only Prettier probe rather than argued, which is what made it decisive.
