# feat/review-round-arc-sum — round economy

The arc that repairs the round-accounting gate reaching its own round cap is the system working rather than a joke at its expense: the filing duty fired mechanically at four counted spec rounds, on the arc whose whole subject is that the duty sometimes does not fire. The fifth round ran past the cap on an explicit orchestrator ruling, as terminal for this stage.

## spec — 5 rounds

**Examined:** R1-R5, 10 findings declared (2, 1, 3, 3, 1), every one admissible under the brief's criterion and every one repaired. Zero refuted. The RULE has not moved since R1 — threshold sums per branch directory over distinct `(baseSha, round)` pairs, filing location stays per-base — and every finding after R1 was about the document's TEST BATTERY under-specifying, with one exception at R4 that was a real hole in the design.

**The one shape worth carrying forward: a rule stated in prose and instantiated by hand.** It cost four of the five rounds, and each time the *reviewer* was right and my *repair* was the next round's defect.

- R2 found the battery could not distinguish a `(branch, stage)` exemption key from one keyed on `branch` alone. I repaired it by writing "one control per coordinate" as prose, then filling the cells myself.
- R3 found two cells I had missed. I had stated the rule correctly and applied it incompletely, which is the enumerated-cover failure on the arc that polices covers. Repaired with a DERIVED matrix: one row per `(key, coordinate)` over all four compound keys, nine cells, a missing control visible as an empty row.
- R3 also opened a second class (values asserted, never presences). I wrote that one as prose too, and listed the values by hand.
- R4 found two values the list omitted, plus a genuine P1 elsewhere. Repaired with a derived inventory: every value the change makes the code compute, each with its by-value assertion. Which I again assembled by hand.
- R5 found a tenth value the inventory omitted — `triggerRateByMonth[month].rate`, a third stored field rendered independently, so a hybrid publishes `213/282  57.8%` with each half individually passing its row. Repaired twice over: the inventory is now derived field-by-field from the `Report` type, and a rendered-line assertion was added that catches struct-versus-rendering disagreement whatever the field list omits.

The lesson is not "state the rule more carefully". It is that **a rule a human applies by hand is not a mechanism**, and the repair for an enumeration defect is a derivation, never a longer list. I needed telling three times, in the same document, having written the rule down myself each time. Both repairs now take that form, and the strongest of them is V11 — assert the rendered line verbatim — because it is the one that stays correct when the enumeration behind it is wrong.

R4's P1 was outside that pattern and is the finding I am most glad of: §3.1 said a clause-B obligation is discharged by a section in "any `.md`" under the directory, but `readArcs` recognizes only `^[0-9a-f]{12}\.md$` and the corpus deliberately ignores stray prose files. A `notes.md` carrying a parseable `## diff` section would have discharged a real obligation while the canonical reader saw no filing at all — an obliged arc reported compliant, the one outcome the consequence bound forbids.

**Mechanizable:**

- "the mutation score's jurisdiction is narrower than the surface it is reported against" (R2, R3) — no operator in the declared set can drop a coordinate from a key expression, so a predicate silently keyed on `branch` alone produces no surviving mutant and costs no score. The durable repair is the derived artifact itself, and both are now in the spec where the next reader of this surface will copy them.

**Judgment:** R1's two findings were a scope call I got wrong in the same document that argued against it — §1.1.6 rejected an advisory tier because a list of 11 unreachable arcs could never drain, and §3.4 then specified a report marking exactly those 11 forever. Reviewer was right; the repair was structural (the report imports the gate's predicate rather than restating it) rather than a patch at the two sites.

**Infra:** none. Five dispatches, five verdicts, no reap, no `no_verdict`.

## plan — 4 rounds

**Examined:** R1-R4. R1-R3 declared eleven findings (5, 4, 2), every one admissible under the brief's criterion and every one repaired; zero refuted. R4 returned `FINDINGS: 0` and APPROVE on the criterion as stated. A first attempt at R4 returned no verdict and is recorded below under Infra.

**The shape that cost this stage its rounds is the one the spec stage had already diagnosed, arriving one layer down.** The spec's filing closed on "a rule a human applies by hand is not a mechanism, and the repair for an enumeration defect is a derivation, never a longer list." The plan then made that mistake about the spec's own derived artifacts.

- **R1** found that the plan NAMED the coordinate-control matrix and the computed-value inventory rather than containing them. A task body pointing at a table in another document is a hand-application of exactly the kind the spec had just finished outlawing: the implementer re-derives it, and re-derivation is where every one of the spec's four lost rounds happened. Repaired by reproducing both tables verbatim in the task section with an owner column, so a cell nobody owns is visible as an empty cell rather than as an absence. R1's other four were ordinary and real: a fixture edit that had silently not applied, a task that could have no possible RED, a mutation command naming a file that never runs `registerSurfaceCases`, and a docs RED that started green.
- **R2** was drift from the R1 rewrite — four stale self-references, a fan-out gap at spec §6 (§5.2 left unreferenced, and §5.2 is the clause ratifying the behaviour this arc repairs), a task claiming AC-9 with a command that reads only the specs tree, and the rendering rule enumerated for one line where four lines render.
- **R3 F1 is the finding worth carrying forward, because it is the derived artifact itself being under-derived.** The matrix was indexed by DATA STRUCTURE, so clause B appeared as one key. Clause B in fact makes three decisions keyed on `(directory, stage)`: the count, the satisfaction lookup, and the suppression. A count-only battery admits four distinct wrong implementations — filings collected globally, the filing's stage ignored, suppression global across directories, suppression global across stages within one — and all four pass every case the plan stated while silently dropping a real obligation. Repaired by re-indexing the matrix **by decision**: K2 became K2a/K2b/K2c, nine cells became thirteen, and the GREEN text now names both scopes, because naming the scope is the thing the control pins.
- **R3 F2** — the V11 row claimed a verbatim rendered-line assertion and had dropped the literal it was meant to assert. A row that names its own mechanism and omits the mechanism's payload.

**Two mechanisms shipped in this branch rather than being filed**, so they are recorded here as done and not as queue entries:

- **A derived table copied into a second document, and the two drift.** R3 F1 is precisely this: the spec's matrix and the plan's disagreed on the key structure. The repair is a check that compares the two on key, coordinate and control, now reporting 13 cells both sides, IDENTICAL. It would have caught R3 F1 before dispatch. The fix for a drift between two hand-maintained copies is a comparison that runs, not a resolution to be careful.
- **A citation that resolves to a line contradicting the sentence citing it.** Found post-APPROVE by self-review, mechanized as sweep 5: derive every `path:line` from the document, resolve it, ECHO the line. Partially mechanizable and labelled as such — the resolver proves the line exists, and a human still compares 26 echoes against 26 claims. Two scanner defects surfaced while building it (a missing lookbehind that matched path suffixes, and blindness to fenced quotations), and both produced false positives indistinguishable from real document defects. Probe the checker before believing it about the document.

Worth noting because it shaped this section: the gate's Mechanizable vocabulary has exactly two dispositions, a cited ledger id or `declined: <reason>`, and no word for "mechanized in this branch" — which is the best available outcome. Writing the two above as `declined:` would have been gate-clean and false. They belong in prose, because a queue of work to schedule should not contain finished work. Recorded as an observation, not filed: a third disposition is a change to the surface this arc is already changing for other reasons, and it would need its own incident evidence under the mint bar.

**Mechanizable:**

- declined: mechanizing "a plan states a rule as prose and fills its instances by hand" means recognizing "one X per Y" in free prose and demanding a table, which is the recognizer-over-prose growth this corpus repeatedly measures as the more expensive defect. The durable repair is the derived artifact itself, and both are now in the documents where the next reader of this surface will copy them.

**Judgment:** R1's central finding was a scope call, and the reviewer was right about it in a way I would not have reached alone. My instinct was that citing a ratified table beats duplicating it, and duplication is normally the defect. It is not the defect when the duplicate is what the implementer's hands are on and the original is two documents away — and the parity check is what makes the duplication safe rather than a second thing to drift.

**The uncomfortable half of R4's APPROVE, recorded so it is not read as a clean bill.** Between dispatch and verdict I found two real defects the reviewer did not raise: `lib/reviewRounds/corpus.ts:124` cited to justify a separator that line does not use, and V11's illustrative rate stale against a corpus that had grown under it. The reviewer was CORRECT not to raise them. R4's consequence bound was narrowed to exactly three things — RED validity, matrix and inventory implementation, AC observability — and neither defect is any of them; under the stated fence they file to documented limits, not to a round. That is the real lesson of this stage's fourth round, and it cuts both ways. Narrowing the bound is what made the round terminate in 490 seconds and return a plain APPROVE instead of a fourth enumeration. Narrowing the bound is also what made it blind to a live class in the document. A bound is not a measurement of the artifact's health; it is a declaration of what this round is about, and everything outside it stays the author's job. Self-review after APPROVE is not belt-and-braces — it is the only thing covering the complement of the bound.

Two things had to be true for that to happen and only one was the wrapper's. The brief carried a five-bullet "where I most expect to be wrong" section, which is the fresh-open-axis pattern the late-arc rule in `AGENTS.md` warns against, and a reviewer handed five axes at the cap round spends the clock enumerating them. The re-dispatch folded the three consequence-bound items into the criterion, dropped the open list, and raised the budget; it finished in 490s, a third of the budget it was given and under a third of what the killed run had burned. The budget was the smaller half of the fix.
