The arc that repairs the round-accounting gate reaching its own round cap is the system working rather than a joke at its expense: the filing duty fired mechanically at four counted spec rounds, on the arc whose whole subject is that the duty sometimes does not fire.

## spec — 4 rounds

**Examined:** R1-R4, 9 findings declared (2, 1, 3, 3), every one admissible under the brief's criterion and every one repaired. Zero refuted. The RULE has not moved since R1 — threshold sums per branch directory over distinct `(baseSha, round)` pairs, filing location stays per-base — and every finding after R1 was about the document's TEST BATTERY under-specifying, not about the design being wrong.

**The one shape worth carrying forward: a rule stated in prose and instantiated by hand.** It cost three of the four rounds, and each time the *reviewer* was right and my *repair* was the next round's defect.

- R2 found the battery could not distinguish a `(branch, stage)` exemption key from one keyed on `branch` alone. I repaired it by writing "one control per coordinate" as prose, then filling the cells myself.
- R3 found two cells I had missed. I had stated the rule correctly and applied it incompletely, which is the enumerated-cover failure on the arc that polices covers. Repaired with a DERIVED matrix: one row per `(key, coordinate)` over all four compound keys, nine cells, a missing control visible as an empty row.
- R3 also opened a second class (values asserted, never presences). I wrote that one as prose too, and listed the values by hand.
- R4 found two values the list omitted, plus a genuine P1 elsewhere. Repaired with a derived inventory: every value the change makes the code compute, nine rows, each with its by-value assertion.

The lesson is not "state the rule more carefully". It is that **a rule a human applies by hand is not a mechanism**, and the repair for an enumeration defect is a derivation, never a longer list. Both repairs now take that form, and both were reached only after the reviewer demonstrated the hand-filled version admitting a wrong implementation.

R4's P1 was outside that pattern and is the finding I am most glad of: §3.1 said a clause-B obligation is discharged by a section in "any `.md`" under the directory, but `readArcs` recognizes only `^[0-9a-f]{12}\.md$` and the corpus deliberately ignores stray prose files. A `notes.md` carrying a parseable `## diff` section would have discharged a real obligation while the canonical reader saw no filing at all — an obliged arc reported compliant, the one outcome the consequence bound forbids.

**Mechanizable:**

- "the mutation score's jurisdiction is narrower than the surface it is reported against" (R2, R3) — no operator in the declared set can drop a coordinate from a key expression, so a predicate silently keyed on `branch` alone produces no surviving mutant and costs no score. Same class as the arithmetic-branch instance already filed, and recorded as this spec's documented limit 8 rather than left as an assumption that `scoreFloor: 1` covers it → BL-MUTATION-SCORE-JURISDICTION-GAP-ARITHMETIC-BRANCH
- "a plan states a rule as prose and fills its instances by hand" (R2/R3, R3/R4) — declined: mechanizing it means recognizing "one X per Y" in free prose and demanding a table, which is the recognizer-over-prose growth this corpus repeatedly measures as the more expensive defect. The durable repair is the derived artifact itself, and both are now in the spec where the next reader of this surface will copy them.

**Judgment:** R1's two findings were a scope call I got wrong in the same document that argued against it — §1.1.6 rejected an advisory tier because a list of 11 unreachable arcs could never drain, and §3.4 then specified a report marking exactly those 11 forever.
