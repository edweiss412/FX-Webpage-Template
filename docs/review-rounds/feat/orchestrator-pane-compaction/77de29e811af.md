## spec — 8 rounds

**Examined:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-16-orchestrator-pane-compaction-design.md`, a
detector-plus-driver surface: a classifier over the live `herdr` pane roster, and commands that
checkpoint and compact a target pane. Rounds 1-7 returned BLOCKING with 10, 9, 8, 6, 5, 7 and 3 findings. **Round 8 returned APPROVE with 0.**
Every finding was accepted; one (round 1's F5, that waiting on `idle` contradicts the measured
terminal `done`) was refuted by probe and the refutation recorded in §1.1 rather than re-argued.

The count was flat for three rounds, fell after the descope, then **rose again**: 10 / 9 / 8 / 6 /
5 / 7 / 3 / 0. The rise at round 6 is the more useful datum. Round 6's seven were not new subject matter — three were
successive races in ONE mechanism (the checkpoint nonce), which had drawn a fresh finding in each
of rounds 4, 5 and 6, each repair widening it and the next round finding the next corner. That is
the ratchet, and the count going up is what made it unmistakable. The flat stretch is the
signature the round-economy retrospective describes, and it was the trigger for treating round 3 as
a scope question rather than a fourth patch. The drop came in the round after that descope, and
round 4's findings changed character with it — six tight mechanical gaps (an unreachable
`--resume` contract, a replayable nonce, a field missing from an accept-set, an undefined
newest-row selection, a rule asserted in three places and implemented in none) rather than design
objections.

**Four narrowings closed thirteen of the forty-eight findings, and no patch closed more than one.**

- Round 2's F3, F4, F5 and limit 1 were four symptoms of the interrupt's race surface, on its
  second round. Removing Esc entirely made all four unreachable. Probe findings P2-P4 retired with
  it.
- Round 3's F5, F6, F7 and F8 were four symptoms of one script owning a multi-hour stateful
  sequence. Decomposing into three one-shot commands closed all four.
- Rounds 4-6 each drew a new race on the checkpoint nonce, which had been accreting toward a
  cross-orchestrator exclusion guarantee. Round 6's repair **dropped the guarantee**: the spec no
  longer claims "exactly one `/compact`" across competing orchestrators, because buying it needs
  consensus across two orchestrators' separate files. The nonce went back to one local job —
  proving this orchestrator's checkpoint landed before this orchestrator sends — and the freshness
  and atomicity machinery came out with the claim. The probe already showed the consequence is
  benign: a second `/compact` on an already-compacted session is a near no-op. Round 7 confirmed
  the narrowing held — it found no remaining cross-orchestrator assertion — and the count fell from
  seven to three.
- The §7 documented-limits preamble drew a finding in rounds 2, 3, 4, 5 and 7: five rounds on one
  sentence. Every repair before the last reworded the offending limit while leaving a blanket
  "each of these is conservative-plus-signaled" quantified over a list that was never homogeneous.
  Round 7's repair **deleted the universal claim** and gave each limit a tier — `[demote]`,
  `[bounded]`, `[residual]` — with §6's enumeration carrying the guarantee instead. The lesson is
  narrow and reusable: a blanket property asserted over a heterogeneous list is a standing invitation
  to a finding per round, and the cheap fix is per-item classification, not better wording.

Both descopes were available at round 1 and were not taken, because each round's findings read as
individually answerable. They were — that is exactly the trap. The signal that should have
triggered the descope earlier was not the severity of any finding but the *arity*: four findings
citing four different sections that all resolve to one mechanism.

**Mechanizable:** `BL-SPECLINT-LINT-DRAFT-OUTSIDE-REPO`. Several defects were mechanically
detectable and were not caught by any existing gate; the dispositions are below.

- **A precedence rule made unreachable by an earlier rule** (round 3 F1: the accept-set classified
  `gh` faults before the rule meant to classify them, making that rule dead code). An ordered rule
  list whose rule N is unreachable is a static property of the spec's own table.
  *declined: reachability depends on what each rule's prose means, not on its shape — a checker
  would need to model the predicates, which is the recognizer-growth trap this corpus documents.*
- **Acceptance criteria not scoped to the precedence they describe** (round 3 F2). AC-4 claimed
  every malformed input is `UNDETERMINED` while AC-5 required an unowned malformed pane to be
  `UNOWNED`.
  *declined: detecting it requires knowing which ACs range over overlapping input sets, which is
  the same semantic modelling as above. One arc is not evidence of a class.*
- **A documented limit contradicting its own section's preamble** (rounds 2 and 3, twice). §7 opens
  "each is conservative-plus-signaled"; limits 1, 2 and 7 each asserted something stronger than
  the body supported.
  *declined: the contradiction is between a claim and its justification, both prose. No mechanical
  form was identified that would not just re-read the prose.*
- **Renumbering drift, twice.** Round 4 renumbered the precedence list to twelve rules; the sweep
  that followed was an ENUMERATED list of fifteen sites and it missed several, which round 5 then
  filed as its own finding. The corpus rule is explicit that a sweep verified by enumeration
  re-opens the moment someone adds a site, and this is a clean instance: the second pass extracted
  the authoritative rule and row tables first and checked every reference against them, which is
  the derived form.
  *declined: the general form needs a checker that knows which rule each reference MEANS, which is
  the same prose-semantics modelling declined above. The derivation is a procedure, not a gate.*
- **The task-marker field order** (`red=` → `red-state=` → `red-target=` → `why=` → `ac=`,
  `lib/specLint/taskContract.ts:49-52`) is fixed, and all ten markers in the plan draft had `ac=`
  before `why=`. Caught by hand against the regex before dispatch; `spec:lint` would have caught
  it, but only once the plan was in-repo — it refuses documents outside the repository, so a
  scratchpad draft cannot be linted. Filed as `BL-SPECLINT-LINT-DRAFT-OUTSIDE-REPO`: this one IS
  mechanical, small, and reusable by every arc that drafts in a scratchpad during a review freeze.

**Judgment:** the shape decisions were not mechanizable and were correctly escalated.

- Whether the driver interrupts. Settled by the three-round prose cap plus a probe showing
  queue-and-wait suffices, not by argument.
- Whether one command owns the sequence or three commands do. Escalated to the user as a scope
  decision after round 3, because "which of several defensible designs ships" is not the run's call.
  The alternative considered and rejected was shipping read-only, which would have closed the same
  four findings but moved the sequence into an agent's judgement where no test can pin it.

**The round-8 brief was the first written to the late-arc rule, and it is the one that closed.**
Briefs for rounds 1-7 each carried a "Where to attack" section. Past the round cap that is the
documented way a brief mints the rounds it predicts, and round 7's named four axes, two of which
the orchestrator opened itself. Round 8's brief names none: it states the bound, the fence and the
probe domain, then says that if nothing is admissible under them, say so and APPROVE. It did. That
is one data point, not a proof — the round-7 repairs were also real — but the rule cost nothing to
follow and the arc had spent seven rounds not following it.

**Infra:** the convergence gate blocked the round-1 dispatch because the brief said "never silently
driven on a wrong verdict" instead of the canonical `correct or signaled, never silently wrong`.
The bound was present and correct in substance; the gate matches literal phrasing. Cheap to fix
once known, and the block happened before any tokens were spent on review — working as designed,
but worth recording that the canonical strings are copied, not paraphrased.

Four self-found repairs (S1, S3, S4, S5) and three more (S7, S8, S9, S10) were staged by probing
the live roster **while review rounds were in flight**, and folded into the next repair commit.
Three of them — `gh` exit-code ambiguity, the session-local out-dir being unobservable from a
worktree, and marker/session mismatch — were defects no round had raised. Probing during the
freeze window cost nothing and removed work from later rounds.

## plan — 4 rounds

**Examined:** `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-16-orchestrator-pane-compaction.md`, ten TDD tasks
implementing the approved spec. Rounds 1-4 returned NEEDS-ATTENTION with 5, 5, 3 and 1 findings;
all fourteen accepted.

**One guard consumed six of the fourteen, and it was a regex.** A structural walker banning
`node:child_process` in `scripts/lib/` drew a distinct hole in four review rounds and two of my own
probes, in this order:

1. use-versus-mention — the bare-specifier pattern matched backticked prose in a comment describing
   the rule, so the guard was red on the untouched corpus (round 2);
2. it also banned `node:fs`, which three files in that directory import legitimately (my probe);
3. a side-effect `import "node:child_process";` walked past a `from`-only matcher (my probe);
4. the bare `child_process` alias, which Node resolves identically, bypassed a `node:`-prefixed
   matcher entirely (round 3);
5. an ordinary **multiline** import, which `[^;\n]*?` cannot span and which occurs throughout the
   live repo (round 4).

Each repair widened the pattern and the next probe found the next corner — the ratchet, in its
textbook form, on a guard whose entire job is nine lines of logic. **Round 4's repair was to stop
pattern-matching and parse**: the guard now uses the TypeScript AST, which the repo already uses
for this class of check. All ten cases pass, positive and negative, and there are no grammar corners
left because the parser resolves the grammar.

**Mechanizable:** none. *declined: the general lesson — "a recognizer over a formal language should
use that language's parser" — is already written in `docs/agents/writing-plans.md` as the
repair-direction rule. This arc is a measured instance of it, not a new rule, and the instance is
worth more than another line of prose would be.*

**Judgment:** the descope decisions were the spec's, not the plan's. The plan's own judgment call
was Task 9's ordering: mutation enrolment must precede the whole-diff review because that review's
brief needs a `GUARD SURFACE:` line carrying a score, which means review repairs land on an
already-enrolled source whose `siteId`s are line-keyed. That trap is documented in
`scripts/print-mutation-sites.ts:9-16` as having cost a prior arc three rounds, and the plan carries
a checklist step rather than an intention.

**Infra:** `spec:lint` refuses out-of-repo documents, so the plan could not be linted while it was
drafted in a scratchpad during a review freeze. Fifteen hard findings surfaced the moment it landed
in the worktree, every one of which would otherwise have been a round-1 plan finding. Filed as
`BL-SPECLINT-LINT-DRAFT-OUTSIDE-REPO`.
