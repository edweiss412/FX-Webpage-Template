## spec — 4 rounds

**Examined:** every finding across the four rounds (4/5/4/3, all verdict rows, none
refuted, none argued away), against what would have caught it earlier, plus the
twelve defects the author's own probing found between dispatches.

**The design curve decayed to zero while the total did not.** Findings on the
DELIVERABLE ran 4 / 4 / 3 / 0; round 4's three were a stale resolved-scope clause,
a miscounted plant attribution, and a coincidental count presented as exact
reproduction — all document fidelity, none touching what the arm does. That is the
corpus's flat-reader-curve shape on a document whose repairs keep touching prose,
and the recorded reading applies: reader rounds cannot outrun what they mint, so the
residual belongs to the executing consumer (the implementer, whose verification is
running the suites) rather than to a fifth reader round.

**Mechanizable:**

- **A hand-rolled markdown reader was the single largest source, and the repo had
  already ratified the answer.** Rounds 1, 2 and 3 each landed a reader defect — the
  optional trailing pipe, the optional leading pipe, backslash parity — and a fourth
  was self-found (a delimiter pattern anchored at column 0 made 106 indented tables
  invisible). The ratification predates this arc by three weeks:
  `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-01-ledger-guard-mdast-rewrite-design.md` §1.1 item 2,
  "Regex reimplementation of markdown grammar is out of scope — the AST port IS the
  sanctioned resolution". `remark` and `remark-gfm` were already declared dependencies
  with a live lib-layer call site at `lib/reviewRounds/filing.ts:60`. Three rounds were
  spent rediscovering a decision the corpus already held.
  **The mechanizable form is a dispatch-time check, not a habit:** a spec that proposes
  to recognize a markdown construct should be refused before dispatch unless it names
  why the parser cannot answer, exactly as `codex-guard` already refuses a round-1 diff
  brief with no `GUARD SURFACE:` score. Filed as the first scheduled step of any
  follow-up to `BL-CODEX-GUARD-SPECLINT-PREDISPATCH-GATE`, which shares the choke point.

- **A number without a generator command drifted twice, in both directions.** Round 1's
  marker count came from a looser population than the command printed; round 2's repair
  tracked the generator but ran `git grep` with no rev, so a staged-but-uncommitted file
  moved 572 to 574 and round 3 caught it. The mechanizable form is narrow and now shipped:
  every count in the document is produced by one tracked script, each section carries the
  exact subcommand, and the marker probe PINS its rev.

**Judgment:**

- **The accept-set was the second axis, and it took three rounds because each repair was
  correct and incomplete in the same direction.** r1: `sh -nc` alone exits 0 on all four
  prose cells the ledger row cited, so the ratified mechanism could not fire on the
  incidents it was filed for. r1's repair (require a code span) admitted a cell whose
  FIRST span parses while a later one does not. r2's repair (every span) collided with a
  results map keyed by line alone, so a broken FIRST span was silently accepted. r3: a
  comment-only span parses. Each round's repair was the right move and opened the next
  round's gap one layer down. What would have caught it earlier is not another rule but
  the question the fourth round finally asked out loud — what, structurally, distinguishes
  a cell that carries a command from one that does not — answered once instead of
  incrementally.

- **A plant that passes by ordering luck is not a plant, and the author shipped one.**
  Round 1's every-span plant broke the SECOND of two spans. Round 2's finding was that a
  line-keyed store loses all but the LAST outcome, so the plant survived the collision by
  accident rather than by the design being right. A second plant covering the other
  ordering was added, and a third plant was found to be pure decoration on inspection
  (removing a leading pipe alone moves nothing, because the row still holds a valid
  command). Plant-both is not satisfied by a plant that moves the criterion; it is
  satisfied by a plant that moves it FOR THE REASON UNDER TEST.

- **Arm (b)'s matcher drew a finding in all three rounds and the repair was to stop
  matching.** A superstring, then a wrong prefix, then quoting and escaped whitespace and
  comment text: every round found another way that identifying a shell ARGUMENT by
  splitting on whitespace is wrong. The close was subtractive — the arm tests a lexical
  path boundary, claims nothing about shell words, and files the quoting families to
  documented limits under the threat fence. Writing a shell lexer for an ADVISORY was
  refused explicitly so a later round cannot reopen it.

- **The instrument was a finding source in its own right, which is the healthy version of
  the guard ratchet.** Four of the arc's defects were in the prototype rather than the
  design, and three of those were found by running it against its own stated wording: its
  corpus filter tested the whole row where the criterion says the last cell, admitting a
  twelfth table and ten spurious findings. A generator that feeds a spec is a spec input
  and gets read against its own claims.

**Round 4's own findings, recorded so a later reader does not re-derive them.** All
three accepted. The one worth keeping is finding 2: "the arm reproduces r2 F4 exactly
(4 of 4)" was an IDENTITY SUBSTITUTION behind a coincidental count. The four caught at
the authored blob are AC-3, AC-5, AC-14 and AC-15; three are r2 F4 instances, the
fourth is the instance r3 F5 raised a round later, and r2 F4's own fourth (AC-12) is
an accepted miss under L-1. A count that matches is not a set that matches, and the
arc had quoted the count for three rounds. Section 4 now states the sets.

**Infra:** none. Every round returned a verdict; no dispatch was reaped, wedged or
usage-limited, and the wrapper recovered no verdict from a rollout.

## plan — 5 rounds

**Examined:** every finding across the five rounds (6/2/2/1/0, all verdict rows, none
refuted, none argued away), plus the three defects the author's own probing found
between dispatches, against what would have caught each earlier.

**The curve decayed to APPROVE and the CLASSES turned over, which is the difference
from the spec stage.** Round 1 was six structural findings — task order, a red that
could not go red, an AC claimed by commands that cannot enforce it, a missing
registry, an accounting gap, three anchor defects. Rounds 2 and 3 were two each, and
by round 3 both were measurement fidelity. Round 4 was one registration gap, and
round 5 returned APPROVE with zero. The stage did not ratchet; it moved down a level
each round, from "the plan's structure is wrong" to "the plan's numbers do not
reproduce" to nothing.

Round 5 was dispatched PAST the four-round cap deliberately, and the reason is part
of the record: the curve was decaying rather than flat, the convergence gate
permitted it, and the plan had never returned APPROVE, so stopping at four would
have meant entering implementation through a gate that had never opened.

**Mechanizable:**

- **A number over a corpus that CONTAINS the document stating it cannot be stable,
  and nothing warned.** Round-3 finding 1: this plan lives in
  `docs/superpowers/plans/`, so its own census moved 929→933 tables between the
  transcript and the review, with both figures correct for the tree they ran on.
  The mechanizable form is narrow: a census whose input set includes the arc's own
  output is pinned to a rev, exactly as the marker probe already is. This is the
  same class as the spec stage's `git grep`-without-a-rev drift, arriving from the
  other direction — there the index moved under the command, here the corpus did.
  One rule covers both: **a generator that reads a moving set names the set's rev.**

- **A sweep whose command cannot produce its stated result.** Round-3 finding 1's
  second half: Sweep B asserted "exactly two documents" while its grep matched a
  third, the spec's own prose describing the grammar. The authored-AND-RUN rule
  already requires the command and its output; what it does not require is that the
  output SATISFY the assertion written beside it. Checking that is mechanical and
  would have caught this at authoring time.

declined: neither candidate is minted as a new row, and the reasons differ. Rev-pinning a
generator that reads a moving set is a HABIT, not a check — a gate would have to know which
generators read moving sets, and deciding that is the recognizer problem this arc just spent
three spec rounds refusing to take on. Re-running a plan's declared sweep commands and
comparing them to the pasted output IS mechanizable, and it is the same choke point as
`BL-CODEX-GUARD-SPECLINT-PREDISPATCH-GATE`, which already refuses a dispatch whose artifact
fails a mechanical gate; it belongs to that row's scope rather than to a second one beside it.
Filing two more process-facing rows against a queue already majority process-facing is the
cost this declines.

**Judgment:**

- **The task reorder was the single most expensive repair, and ordinals were why.**
  Round 1's repair renumbered eleven tasks into twelve; round 2 then found 19 stale
  references, thirteen of them in the AC table. The corpus rule about fencing
  content rather than ordinals is right, and the durable repair was not to fix
  nineteen numbers but to DERIVE the AC table's owning-task column from the markers'
  `ac=` fields, so the two cannot disagree again. A hand-fix of nineteen ordinals is
  how a twentieth appears.

- **The arm this arc is building would have reported its own plan clean.** Round 2's
  sharpest observation, and it is a limit rather than a defect: `acCoverage` checks
  the producing-command cell and says nothing about whether the proving task exists
  or owns that criterion. Recorded in the spec rather than repaired, because closing
  it means reading the plan's task structure, which is a different arm.

- **Probe-a-member is PER ARTIFACT KIND, and running it well for one kind is what
  makes skipping the second feel safe.** Round-1 finding 4: the fan-out was probed
  thoroughly for the lint-arm kind — nine sites, one of them compiler-enforced — and
  not at all for the mutation-surface kind, which cost `EXPECTED_LEDGER_KINDS`. The
  rule was already written down in this repo's lessons in exactly those words. This
  is its second recorded instance.

- **A guard cited for an AC it cannot enforce is worse than no guard cited.**
  Round-1 finding 3: AC-15 claimed the pure core admits no third-party import and
  pointed at a walker that forbids three `node:` modules. The AC would have gone
  green while the boundary the spec had just settled went unguarded. The repair adds
  the assertion and PLANTS its red, because a guard authored green proves nothing.

- **Registration completeness produced a finding in half the rounds, and the misses
  were in DIFFERENT registries each time.** Round 1 missed `EXPECTED_LEDGER_KINDS`
  for the mutation-surface kind; round 4 missed the `Check` union and the exported
  `CHECK_ORDER` array for the lint-arm kind, because the plan named `run.ts`'s
  ordering map and there are TWO `CHECK_ORDER`s. Both times the prose ASSERTED a
  probe had been run. The lesson is not "probe harder" but that a probe's OUTPUT
  belongs in the plan: round 4's repair pastes the `git grep` and marks which sites
  are compile-enforced, which is the form round 1's repair had already used for the
  mutation kind and which round 4 then showed missing for the lint-arm kind.

  Worth recording precisely because the failure is quiet: vitest strips types, so
  all three compile-enforced sites can be absent while every task-level suite
  passes, and the omission surfaces at typecheck or in CI. `pnpm typecheck` is now
  an explicit step of the task that lands them.


**Infra:** none. Every round returned a verdict; no dispatch was reaped, wedged or
usage-limited. One liveness scare was resolved by measurement rather than a kill: a
stderr stream flat for 36 seconds resumed on the next 40-second sample, which is the
gap-between-bursts reading, not stationarity.
