## diff — 4 rounds

**Examined:** whole-diff rounds 1–4 (r1 NEEDS-ATTENTION 1, r2 NEEDS-ATTENTION 3, r3 NEEDS-ATTENTION 3, r4 NEEDS-ATTENTION 2). Every round returned findings and every finding was accepted — none was refuted, and none was a "the guard does not pin what it claims" round on prose. Round 4 found a key MISMATCH between the ledger's two sides: `event.ts` marked rows under the SEMANTIC `EVENT DETAILS` header while the detector probes the PHYSICAL pipe-run opener, so a resolved, autocorrected `Stage-Size` was simultaneously reported unrecognized under a neighbouring block's namespace on four corpus fixtures. Both sides now derive the opener from one function (`openerByLine`), so they cannot disagree. Two are worth naming because they were live product defects the whole spec-and-plan train had missed: r2's count-vs-set ledger read (`markConsumed` increments a count, `detectFieldNearMisses` asked `.has()`, so two rows sharing the trimmed triple were both silenced by one resolution — a near-miss going silent, one copy/paste from the committed corpus), and r3's unledgered fuzzy resolutions (a row the parser understood, populated a field from, and autocorrected was ALSO reported unrecognized). Both were probe-backed by the reviewer against the live tree, both inside the brief's declared probe domain, and both are the same defect class read from opposite sides: the ledger's write side and its read side disagreeing about what "resolved" means.

**Mechanizable:** (a) **ledger-count claims in prose** — three consecutive rounds (r1 one site, r2 five, r3 six) found stale `10`/`72` section-reorder counts, each repair an enumeration that missed the next file. Now derived: `tests/docs/waveLedgerCountClaims.test.ts` reads the count from the live ledger, walks the wave documents from disk, and fails on a contradicting claim unless the line marks itself superseded. It found a seventh site on its first run, which is both the argument for it and the measure of what enumeration was costing. (b) **the shrink instrument itself** — `knownHoles.ts` already carried branch 4's rule ("size a shrink by the harness's own `fixedHoles` set, never by an operator's row count"), and this arc shipped a plan-authored id-set anyway and burned a full 2-hour harness cycle discovering the closure was 24, not 10, plus 62 more across three operators nobody was aiming at. A plan-time step that runs `COLLECT_MUTATION_ALARMS` BEFORE authoring the deletion list would have made the ten a non-event.

**Judgment:** r3's fuzzy-resolution finding needed reading three block parsers against a spec sentence about resolution-site semantics and noticing that `FIELD_LABEL_AUTOCORRECTED` and `UNKNOWN_FIELD` are mutually contradictory claims about one row. No static check reaches that — it is a statement about what two warnings MEAN together. Bought rounds, not burned ones.

**Infra:** none this stage. All four dispatches returned verdicts under the 1380s budget recipe; the reviewer's own probes ran read-only against the live tree, and it reported honestly where a sandbox `EPERM` blocked Vitest collection rather than asserting a suite result it did not have.
