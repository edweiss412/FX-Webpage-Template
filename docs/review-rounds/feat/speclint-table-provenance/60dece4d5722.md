# Review rounds — `feat/speclint-table-provenance`, base `60dece4d5722`

## spec — 4 rounds

**Examined:** all 4 counted spec rounds (R1 4, R2 5, R3 4, R4 6), plus one uncounted
`no_verdict` (`total_timeout`, both attempts SIGTERMed, re-dispatched at the same stage and round
per the wrapper contract). Every finding was accepted; none was disputed or argued down. The arc
declines to build the lint its row asked for, so the rounds were spent almost entirely on whether
the evidence for declining is true, not on what to build.

**The arc became the twelfth naming of the class it was declining to mechanize.** That is the
finding worth carrying forward, and it cuts against the reflex to read it as vindication for the
row. Across four rounds the reviewer charged, repeatedly, exactly two shapes:

1. **A figure stated with nothing producing it.** R1 F1 (the census printing a retyped "6 rounds
   across four namings" while the spec's methodology claimed every figure came from that command),
   R2 F2 (the §8 population measured by a scratch script that had been deleted), R2 F5 (a quoted
   precedent with no `file:line`), R3 F2 (eight of eleven repair rows carrying a quotation and no
   location).
2. **A universal claimed wider than its evidence.** R1 F3 ("every paying arc removed the
   hand-written table" — one kept it and committed the command's output beside it), R2 F1 ("six of
   eleven shipped a repair" — all eleven did, and "shipped EVIDENCE" does not cover deletion).

**Round 4 charged both shapes again, and one of them for the third time on the same argument.** F1 was
an off-by-one inside the census itself: fence spans were 0-based array indexes compared against 1-based
mdast table lines, so every adjacency figure was quietly wrong (10/23/55/99, not 12/22/53/98; the pure
population 11/3/8 was unaffected). Silent corruption inside the declared probe domain, in the probe
whose subject is unverifiable numbers. F2 retired the second argument's third draft as tautological.

**It does NOT vindicate the marker, and the reason is the whole spec in miniature.** Not one of
those defects would have been caught by a `<!-- table: cmd=`…`` -->` marker. Most were prose
figures and stale counts, which no table-bound marker sees; the one genuine table-versus-command
instance, the census's own hardcoded sentence, was caught by a reviewer reading the document, not
by any check. What DID fix them is the repair the eleven arcs had already converged on and which
§5 records: stop writing the number down and produce it. The naming population is now emitted by
the census and classified in the spec; the §8 candidate population likewise. Both had been hand
enumerations, and both were wrong while they were.

**Mechanizable:** the class these rounds hit is `LIM-NUMERIC-TABLE-PROVENANCE` itself, whose
mechanization this arc measured and declined.

declined: recording it as newly mechanizable here would be the arc contradicting its own §5 in its own
paperwork. The measurement is in `docs/superpowers/specs/ci/2026-08-28-table-provenance.md` §5 and §6,
and the slug's `docs/review-rounds/LIMITS.md` entry carries the disposition with a narrowed re-file
trigger. The repair that works on this class is authoring discipline plus a producing command, already
ratified in `docs/superpowers/specs/ci/probes/README.md` under `## Stating a figure`; what this arc
added is that the practice is common and still insufficient, which is a reason not to mechanize it
rather than a new thing to build.

**Judgment:** the disposition, and it moved twice under measurement without changing. Three of the
four drafted arguments weakened or inverted during review — the cost leg inverted outright once the
naming count was derived rather than counted from slug citations (4 arcs to 11, 6 rounds to ~20),
the population leg became an upper bound, and the adoption leg turned out to rest on a marker three
days old. The spec now opens with a ledger saying so. What none of that touched is the structural
argument: a producing command is not a binding, so a marked table can satisfy the check and be
exactly as drifted. An expensive problem is not a reason to ship a mechanism that cannot solve it,
and that call is the judgment the rounds did not mechanize.

**Infra:** one, and it changed the process rather than the artifact. Round 1's first dispatch
returned `no_verdict` / `total_timeout` after spending its entire budget re-running the census, at
roughly 90 seconds a run, once per figure it wanted to check. Treated as an infrastructure fault
and re-dispatched at the same stage and round to a fresh out-dir. The repair was to paste the
census's full output into the brief and tell the reviewer to run it at most once; every subsequent
round returned a verdict on a single attempt in about 370 seconds. A reviewer asked to verify by
re-measurement will spend its budget measuring.
