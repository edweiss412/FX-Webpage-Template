# Review-round economy — `test/modal-wait-helper-adoption` @ `119895a7c756`

## spec — 7 rounds

**Examined:** all seven spec rounds (2 / 2 / 2 / 3 / 2 / 1 / 0 findings; BLOCKING through R6,
APPROVE at R7), their briefs, and the repair commits `904954e15`, `cea13d308`, `2b1ff1f9b`,

**Judgment:** ten of the twelve findings were real design gaps that could only have been found
by reading the corpus against the mechanism. The other two (both in R4) were internal
contradictions introduced by an earlier round's own repair — cheap, and the one genuinely
repeatable saving available here. The census-format lesson is under **Mechanizable** below.

The twelve, by what they cost:

- **R1-1 / R2-1 / R3-1 / R4-1 are one vector — census completeness — seen four times.** Each round
  found a different way for a test to reach `/admin?show=` that the previous derivation could
  not see: a FILE-keyed census hiding nine sites inside four specs (R1), a `page.reload()` that
  writes no URL for any URL-keyed grep to match (R2), and two sites whose skeleton-tolerant
  wait means the helper would return on the skeleton and hide the fault rather than surface it
  (R3), and the product-surface enumeration built to close the vector being itself incomplete
  (R4). Three rounds on one vector is the trigger AGENTS.md defines, and R3's repair was the
  mandated one — comprehensive re-analysis plus a structural defense, not a fourth patch. It
  was not enough, and R4 is why: the re-analysis produced a prose table, and a prose table over
  a live tree is the format that had already failed three times. See **Mechanizable**.
- **R2-2 and R3-1 were both mechanism findings a probe settles and prose cannot.** Playwright's
  `isVisible`/`waitFor` are strict, so a multi-match ready selector throws an exit naming
  neither the boundary nor the server signature; and the Suspense skeleton renders through the
  same shell with the same `testIdBase`, so it WINS a modal-or-boundary race. Both were
  confirmed from source in minutes once named. Neither was reachable from the spec text alone.
- **R1-2 is the one finding with real leverage.** The guard's file-level import predicate would
  have shipped a false-negative machine: one adopted site silences every other site in the same
  file. It was refuted with a live escape (`published-review-modal.interactions.spec.ts` adopts
  at 103, its naked 389 goes quiet) and the repair changed the design rather than the
  predicate — adding a URL-taking entry point so no spec writes the navigation at all, which
  lets the guard be a flat per-site ban instead of a heuristic.

**Mechanizable:** one, and it is the census FORMAT rather than any individual miss.

All four rounds landed a finding on census completeness. Round 3 triggered the same-vector
rule, so round 3's repair shipped the mandated comprehensive re-analysis — a product-keyed
table of every surface whose href targets the route. Round 4 then found THAT table incomplete:
it miscounted itself ("eight across six components" over nine rows across seven), and it still
omitted `ShowRowActions.tsx` 694 and 1003, `StagedReviewCard.tsx:372`'s
`router.push(openHref(slug))`, the crew-side link in `app/show/[slug]/unpublish/blocks.tsx`, and
every dynamic `action.href` built in `lib/adminAlerts/alertActions.ts` and rendered by
`BellPanel` and `HealthAlertsPanel`.

That fourth finding is the arc's real lesson, and it is not "the table was sloppy." A more
careful table would have been the fifth attempt at a format that had already failed four times.
The rule AGENTS.md states for this case — a finding on the same vector AFTER the comprehensive
re-analysis means the analysis was wrong in KIND — is exactly right here, and the structural
repair is to stop enumerating in prose at all:

- The candidate list became CODE (test-corpus walk + a product scan for `openHref(` call sites,
  literal hrefs, and `action.href` builders), with nothing typed into the document.
- The assertion became a TOTAL DISPOSITION — `members ∪ exclusions = candidates`, exactly,
  undispositioned fails. Round 4 identified this precisely: a subset assertion is vacuous
  against a newly-omitted candidate, and a bare equality can never pass because four of the five
  origins deliberately yield non-members. Neither half of that is obvious, and the earlier
  AC-2b promised divergence-detection it could not have delivered.
- The prose tables were demoted to non-normative renderings, which retires
  "the table is incomplete" as a category of finding.

**The generalizable rule:** when a spec's central artifact is an enumeration OVER A LIVE TREE,
it belongs in code from the first draft and the prose table is its rendering. Four rounds is
what it cost to learn that here; the trigger to watch for is a second round finding a new member
of the same enumeration, not a fourth.

Two counter-notes against over-learning it. First, the R3 exclusions — two sites whose
skeleton-tolerant wait means the helper would hide the fault rather than surface it — would NOT
have been caught by any executable enumeration. They are members by every mechanical test and
are excluded on a semantic judgment about what those two tests assert. Second, total disposition
closes candidate omission but not ORIGIN omission; the spec now says so as a documented limit
rather than implying the class is fully closed.

The other two R4 findings were ordinary and cheap, and both are the self-consistency sweep the
authoring rules already mandate, applied between rounds rather than only at draft time: AC-3
still demanded one pinned guard exemption after §2.2 and §4.4 had moved to two, and §4.4's
"38 goto lines" partition still described the pre-exclusion split. Both were introduced by MY
OWN previous round's edit. That is the same between-round-sweep gap other filings in this corpus
record, and it is the one genuinely repeatable saving available here.

**Decay:** the finding rate did decay once the census left the document — 2 / 2 / 2 / 3 / 2 / 1 / 0,
with R5 and R6 both narrow and both closing on the first attempt. That is worth recording against
this corpus's usual pattern, where BLOCKING-per-round stays flat into the twenties: the arcs that
stay flat are the ones whose deliverable keeps growing to meet the reviewer. Here the deliverable
SHRANK at R4 (a table was deleted, not rewritten) and again at R3 (the `readySelector` option was
removed rather than hardened), and the rate fell immediately after.

**R5 and R6, briefly.** R5-1 was drift I introduced myself: R3 widened the guard to match a
`show` param in any position and left derivation origin (a) on the literal spelling, so the guard
flagged an input the candidate producer never proposed and the total disposition was not total.
The repair is one shared exported constant with a unit case pinning that both consumers use it —
two regexes that happen to agree is exactly what had just failed. R5-2 was a genuine contract
collision: AC-1 asked a fixture to prove the post-recovery re-wait, which lives inside the branch
documented limit 2 says has no deterministic test. Narrowing AC-1 to the locator's CARDINALITY
resolved it and is strictly stronger per unit of machinery, since both waits use that locator.

R6-1 is the one I would most want a future arc to copy. `if: always()` gets a gating oracle RUN
but not its print REACHED, because the oracle exits above its reporting tail. The reviewer named
the crew oracle; the sweep found `check-app-e2e-executed.mjs` has the identical shape, so the
parent arc's ALREADY-SHIPPED recovery print was unreachable on exactly the failure it exists to
report. The repair is ordering by construction — the gating exit is the last statement — rather
than a rule anyone has to remember, and it preserves the green-run stdout pin byte-for-byte.
