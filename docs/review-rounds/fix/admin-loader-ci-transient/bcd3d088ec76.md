## spec — 5 rounds

**Examined:** R1-R4 of the `spec` stage on `fix/admin-loader-ci-transient`, their briefs, and the
repair commit each produced (`64bbd5f73`, `b5801610c`, `ac022c656`, `b737c5e5d`, `f8fbf4f11`, `8a8ba0bb5`, and
R5's). Declared findings ran 6, 1, 2, 4, 5. A fifth dispatch exists in the corpus as `no_verdict`: it was killed
mid-flight because the arc found a defect in the very section that dispatch was reviewing, and a
reviewer on a superseded tree is a spent round. Two further defects were found by the arc in its own
artifact while a review was in flight, and are counted here because they cost the same repair cycles.

**Mechanizable:** declined: the class is real and its lint's accept-set is not specifiable today (the
reasoning is below the instance list). One class produced R2, R3's blocking finding, R4's blocking
finding, and one self-found defect, and it is
the same rule restated three times at increasing range: **a completeness claim keyed on a call site
rather than on an external authority.**

- R2: discovery scoped to files importing the factory. The client travels as a value, so a
  parameter-passed client was invisible; the same file's COMMENTS matched the scan. Under- and
  over-inclusive at once.
- Self-found: discovery keyed on `.rpc("<name>")`. A helper takes the name as a parameter, so the
  literal lives at a different call shape entirely.
- R3: the §7 dump keyed on the wrapper's own retry emit. An emit exists only where the wrapper runs,
  so four RPCs on the excluded client could fault with the job green and nothing recorded.
- R4: the repair for R3 keyed on the fault STRING instead, which then missed every fault a retry
  successfully absorbed, because the emit does not carry Kong's body. Same class, opposite direction,
  and the fix for one had broken the other.
- R5: the transport observer that replaced all of it re-opened the §6.1 recursion fence, because the
  durable log sink persists `warn` records THROUGH the very service-role client the observer had just
  been extended to. The fence had been written down one round earlier, about the WRAPPER, and did not
  survive being restated about the OBSERVER. The final repair moves the fence onto a property of the
  log LEVEL (`debug` can never persist, by a CHECK constraint and a pinned test) rather than onto a
  client scope that any later change can widen.

Each repair was the same move: stop asking a call site or a consumer to reveal something, and read it
where it is structurally available instead. The final form is the clearest: the fault is logged at the
TRANSPORT, which every call passes and no consumer can swallow, which dissolved R3's and R4's findings
together and made the diff SMALLER by dropping four consumer edits. 

**Why declined rather than filed.** The obvious arm is a lint flagging a completeness or trigger predicate written as a syntactic or local
match when an external enumeration is available for the same question. Filing it would mean minting a
process-facing row whose matcher cannot be stated, and a detector with an undefined accept-set is the
exact shape this corpus keeps punishing. Writing one to catch that shape would be self-refuting.

What the incident supports instead is the already-ratified rule it instantiates: keep completeness
predicates keyed on an external authority, which AGENTS.md carries as the property-over-recognizer
discipline. Re-file trigger: a fourth independent instance in a DIFFERENT arc, at which point the
accept-set can be derived from worked examples rather than guessed. This arc's three are all one
surface, which is a weak basis for a repo-wide matcher.

**Judgment:** R5's census finding is the sharpest instance of the arc's other recurring error, and it
is also not mechanizable: `auth_email_canonical` was classified service-role because its FILE builds a
service-role client, while the RPC is issued a few lines later on a different client. File-level
attribution cannot answer a per-CALL question. R4 also caught a scope conflation no lint would: §7.1's table was drawn from the RETRY
population (non-`VOLATILE` RPCs) when the INSTRUMENT's population is strictly larger — any call that
can 502 and be swallowed, VOLATILE RPCs and table reads included. The two sets differ, and the
overlap is large enough that the conflation survived three rounds. R1's strongest finding is likewise
not mechanizable. A retry loop surfacing the LAST attempt
silently rewrites the caller-visible error CATEGORY on mixed failures, and establishing it required
reading ten consumer branches and knowing each emits a different forensic code for returned-vs-thrown.
The repair (replay the FIRST attempt on exhaustion) is a design decision, not a pattern. R3's census
findings are also judgment-adjacent: the defect was an unstated SCOPE, and the fix was to state it.

**Infra:** none. Four dispatches, four verdicts, no `no_verdict`, no reap, no wrapper fault. The
convergence gate blocked one dispatch correctly (the R3 brief was the first to mention a guard, which
armed a check the earlier briefs never triggered) and the brief was repaired rather than overridden.

**Against the obvious lesson.** Four rounds reads as two too many, and the corpus should record why it
is not. The finding COUNT fell 6, 1, 2 while the finding SIZE stayed load-bearing to the last round:
R3's blocking finding was a silent-attribution hole that would have shipped an instrument going dark
exactly as the repair started working. Nothing in R1-R4 was a style round. What the count does
understate is how much convergence was internal: two of the defects were found by the arc's own
sweeps before the reviewer reported, and one of those (the parameterized-helper hole) was found by
probing the arc's OWN brief's top attack question.
