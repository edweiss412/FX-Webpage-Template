**Judgment:** nine of the thirteen findings were ONE vector — how to exempt the switch tracks —
and it ran the entire five rounds before being resolved by deletion. Five mechanisms, five
structural escapes, no decay: R5's escape composes two patterns already live in the repo. What
the vector was really saying is that the question "is this element a switch track" is about
rendered structure and effective paint, and `scanInteractiveElements` reports neither — it
reports an element's own statically-resolvable class strings. Every mechanism recovered a
slightly larger subset of structure from that projection and left the next mutation available.
The resolution was the AGENTS.md kill: cut the classifier from the arc, ship a regression pin
over the enumerated 21 (which needs no classification at all), and file the forward-guard
ambition with all five closed escapes as its evidence.

**R6 is the other lesson, and it is about the repair rather than the design.** Cutting the
classifier in R5 left five stale references to it in §§2, 4.2, 5.4, 6 and 7 — the document
claimed a registry and a forward guarantee that no longer existed anywhere. That is the
"repair's own tidy-up is a defect site" rule, and the sweep that would have caught it is the one
this corpus already mandates on every finding. R6 also caught an arity that was wrong in a new
way (22 source edits, not 23: two elements share a file-local constant), a §5.3 obligation the
rewrite silently dropped while two other sections still cited it, and — the one that stings — a
mutation precedent cited to the wrong module: the zero-mutant note belongs to
`subtleInteractiveScan.ts`, while `tapTargetScan.ts` is the row immediately after it and IS
enrolled at `scoreFloor: 0.9`. A precedent argument built on a misread is worse than no
precedent, and the corrected one happens to be a closer shape match.

**R7 is one line long and worth more than its size: the R6 sweep was run through `cut -c1-120`.**
The stale claim it missed — "the cover and the exemption predicate live in an importable module"
— sat in the second half of a line whose first half read "with the classifier cut, what remains
is a regression pin". The sweep printed the line, the reviewer's own grep printed the line, and
the truncation hid the contradiction inside it. **A sweep that truncates its output has not been
run.** Pipe through `fold`, not `cut`, or read the matches whole; this cost a full round to learn
on a document whose every other claim had already been verified six times.

**The arc-level lesson, stated against itself: the kill was available at R3 and was taken at R5.**
R3 already had three escapes and already invoked the narrowing rule — but narrowed twice more
(accept-set, then file registry) before reading its own evidence. The rule names three repair
directions and this arc tried them in the most expensive order. A cheaper trigger: **when the
second mechanism for one question falls to a structural escape, price the kill before the third
mechanism** — especially when, as here, the guard is optional to the ruling and the product
change is 22 token edits (this said 23 at spec stage; the arc's own line 4 and every later
measurement say 22 — whole-diff R11). The predicate
went single-token → existential two-branch → universal accept-set, and each form was broken by
one ordinary edit: a replaced token, an appended branch, a conflicting same-branch token. R3 F2
is where it became clear the derivation is not soundly achievable at all — exactness requires
deciding which of two paint tokens on one branch wins the cascade, i.e. a CSS evaluator inside a
test helper. The repair was the AGENTS.md narrowing rule applied literally: kill the derived
predicate, enumerate a five-row registry, and narrow the guard's claim to something decidable by
token presence alone. **The cost of learning that was three rounds, and the honest read is that
two of them were avoidable** — the trajectory (one new input family per round) was visible after
R2, and the rule that names it was already written. The trigger to apply it should be "the
second round on one recognizer", not the third.

R1 F1 was the other genuine one: the cover is not a cover. `<input>`/`<textarea>` are outside
the scanner's element vocabulary for the shapes this arc cares about — `interactiveScanCore.ts:868`
admits an `<input>` at `type="checkbox"`/`"radio"` and nothing else, so a number field and a
textarea are invisible to it while nine checkboxes and radios are not. (Written as "absent
... entirely" at spec stage; the spec itself corrected that at §3.2a R4 F2 and this filing did not
follow, which whole-diff R11 caught.) Separately, an outline painted on a nested child leaves the
interactive element's own class list clean. That is a property of `interactiveScanCore` that no
amount of reading this spec would surface — it took a probe — and it is why the arc's bound is
now quantified over what the cover can see, with the residue filed rather than claimed.

**Note for the next arc that builds a class-string guard.**

The three broken predicates are kept in spec §5.2 as a table with their mutants, specifically so
the derivation is not re-attempted without them. The short version: **a predicate over Tailwind
class strings can decide token PRESENCE soundly and cannot decide EFFECTIVE PAINT soundly**,
because effective paint is a cascade question and the class list does not carry the cascade. Any
guard whose claim needs the second is the wrong shape, and the round count will say so one input
family at a time.
