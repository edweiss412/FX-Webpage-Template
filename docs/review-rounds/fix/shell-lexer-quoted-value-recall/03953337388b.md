**All four rounds landed on ONE axis: what arm 2's model of a `${…}` value can be, and how far it
reaches.** R1 an attached-redirection family; R2 two unnamed operators, substring expansion, and a

**Mechanizable:** none of the three rounds could have been prevented by a lint rule available today,
but two of them were preventable by a DERIVATION the arc did not perform at draft time:

- The operator partition was authored as a list. A list over a grammar admits one more round

  complement — a form that could have been written at draft time at no extra cost.
- The declared-limit pin inventory was authored from a PHRASE GREP and reported as complete. It was
  short by one, and the missing pin was invisible to that method by construction: it asserts a
  degraded LINE, not a zero, so no amount of care with the grep output would have found it. A confident
  clean result from an unsound method reads exactly like a sound one at authorship time.

**Judgment (1 of 3):** R3's composition finding is the one no derivation would have caught. It is a defect in the
MODEL — an array of recorded operands cannot see a value assembled from an operand plus adjacent text,
because neither part binds alone. Finding that required someone to ask what a word IS rather than what
it contains. The repair replaced the model with one substituted candidate and is smaller than what it
replaced; composition, nested composition and the R2 separator problem all became consequences instead
of specified behavior.

**Judgment (3 of 3):** R5's single finding is the one worth reading twice, because its SHELL SEMANTICS
were right and its ATTRIBUTION was wrong, and only a probe in the reviewer's own units could tell them
apart. It said the design reads an accepted operand without regard to the parameter's unset/null state
and thereby creates false reports. True about the reading; false about the arc. The scanner reports 1
TODAY for `U=other` + `PG=${U:-psql}` and for `PG=${U:+psql}` with `U` unset, while bash binds `other`
and empty — so the unconditional read is ratified behavior that shipped long before this arc, and arm 2
extends the identical treatment from bare operands to quoted ones. What made the finding land against
the document was a phrase THIS ARC had tightened one round earlier: R4's repair restated the
consequence bound as "never reports a binding bash does not make", which condemns the guard's own
ratified may-bind posture. The lesson is narrow and mechanical: **a consequence bound is a claim about
the surface, so tightening its wording is a change to the surface's contract and owes the same probe
any other claim does.** An over-tight bound does not make a guard better; it makes correct behavior
indefensible.

**Judgment (2 of 3):** R4 is the round that settles the economics rather than adding another instance.
Three of its five findings were consequences of R3's repair rather than of the original design, which
is the documented pattern that the repair is the next round's most likely defect — here it produced an
outright contradiction between two guarantees stated four paragraphs apart. At that point the axis is
not converging by patching, the arc is at its 4-round cap, and the disposition (narrow arm 2 to
whole-value expansions and let composition go back to being a documented limit, or split the arms
across two PRs) is a SCOPE decision that belongs to the orchestrator. It was escalated rather than
decided in-arc, with the run left live.

**Infra:** three facts cost measurable time and are worth recording for the next arc on this surface.

containing psql spellings put the deciding suite RED and the mutation gate into

green from birth and must never be used as a red or a gate; the `--project mutation` +
`VITEST_INCLUDE_MUTATION_HARNESS=1` form is the one that collects.

## plan — 4 rounds

**Examined:** all four plan dispatches. Findings ran 6, 4, 3, 2 — decreasing every round, which is the
shape the spec stage never had. All fifteen were accepted; none was refuted.

**Round 1 was one defect with instances, not six defects.** Every finding traced to the same mistake:
tasks whose RED could not be observed at their own boundary. Retention was authored as its own task
although `lexShellWords` is private and nothing consumed its output until the next task; the negative
surface was authored as a task of zeros that must stay zero, which cannot red against correct code;
and pin retirement was deferred past the tasks that invalidate the pins, so the suite would have been
red at every intermediate commit. The repair was a re-decomposition, not six patches.

**Mechanizable:** two of the four rounds are already covered by rules this repo owns and the arc simply
did not apply them at authoring time — the RED-validity rule (a red must name the production line whose
defect makes it fail, and must not be satisfiable by editing the test) and the premise rule (a premise
is proven on the case's OWN inputs; an adjacent case is not a premise). Round 3's finding 3 was the
premise rule verbatim, and round 1's findings 1 and 2 were RED-validity verbatim. A plan-lint arm that
checked "does this task's red-target line exist AND is the task's first step authoring a case against
it" would have caught the first; nothing mechanical would have caught the second, because "this
premise is about a sibling" is a semantic reading.

**Judgment:** the highest-value finding in the stage was round 2's finding 3, and it was a claim the
arc had PROBED and still got wrong. The plan asserted that `scanShellIndirection` emits at most one hit
per line — true of the coalesced line route, false of the function, because `visitBody` emits
independently and earlier. The arc had even measured a two-hit case early in the spec stage
(`X=$(read -r PG <<< psql)`) and did not connect it. The consequence was subtle and exactly the class
the cross-finding rule exists for: A7's premise could have been satisfied by nested DISCOVERY rather
than by the here-string route under test, so a fixture would have gone green proving nothing. **A probe
that is read for its headline number and not for its shape is not evidence yet.**

**Infra:** the strictly-weaker-implementation table needed three rounds to become sound — round 1
replaced three of five killers, round 2 replaced another. The recurring error was proposing a weaker
implementation that fails the plan's POSITIVE fixtures rather than passing them, which makes the row
vacuous: `${U:1}` is not a killer for a denylist because the denylist extracts the operand `1` and the
predicate rejects it either way, so both implementations agree. A killer only discriminates when the
two implementations DISAGREE on that exact input, and checking that is a second step the first two
drafts skipped.
