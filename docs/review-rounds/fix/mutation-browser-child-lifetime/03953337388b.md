## spec — 1 round

**Examined:** the design for `BL-MUTATION-BROWSER-CHILD-LIFETIME` — a per-child wall-clock ceiling for
the browser mutation gate, derived from a measurement this arc took rather than from the source
harness's constant.

**Judgment:** one round at zero findings is the outcome the measurement bought. The spec's contested
decision (a timeout maps to INFRA, not KILLED) shipped with its counter-argument written out, and the
ceiling arrived as a stated multiple of a reproduced measured maximum rather than as a number. The
reviewer independently recomputed the arithmetic and returned a refinement rather than a finding — the
job-budget table was CONSERVATIVE, since the first timeout aborts the invocation and a second hung
child is unreachable. Folding that in tightened the bound to an exact 2460 s against 3600 s.

## plan — 4 rounds

**Examined:** the task breakdown implementing that design — the ceiling constant, the call-site swap
to `spawnBounded`, the fallback assertion, and the class sweep.

**Mechanizable:** two of the four rounds were spent on ONE shape that a tool could have caught, and it
recurred after being named. **A test that fails before its assertions run is not a RED.** Round 1
found it in Task 1 (an unresolved import); round 2 found it again in Task 2 (`runChild` is private, so
the suite fails on missing ACCESS). The plan had a section rejecting exactly this shape two headings
above the task that committed it. `spec:lint --exec-red` cannot see either case: the arm is silent for
`pnpm heavy`-wrapped commands (`lib/specLint/redContract.ts:721`) and makes no collection claim at all
when probes do not run (`:754`). An arm that ran the red and compared the failure to the task's stated
reason would have caught both. Filed as `BL-SPECLINT-RED-REASON-VERIFICATION`.

**Judgment:** the other three findings were genuine design work and are the reason the round count was
worth paying.

- **AC-6 across rounds 2 and 3 — the productive axis.** Round 2 showed the `perl`-absent fallback
  ceiling was unasserted. Round 3 showed the obvious repair was still too weak: an implementation
  OVERWRITING the direct spawn's ceiling with `MUTANT_TIMEOUT_MS` passes a presence check, the
  existing fixture, and the closeout gate — because that gate runs with `perl` PRESENT and never
  exercises the fallback. The consequence was a live wrong ceiling (180 s instead of the ratified
  660 s on a `perl`-absent host), reachable and silent. The repair converted a presence check into a
  discriminating one — caller-value forwarding, pinned by two mutants chosen so that presence-only
  kills the first and not the second.
- **Round 4 — an unsound sweep.** The class sweep piped through `grep -v '\.test\.ts'`, excluding
  members of its own class by filename before inspecting them, while claiming walk-root coverage. It
  therefore could not have established its "one unrepaired member" conclusion. Removing the filter
  surfaced four more in-class sites in `tests/mutation/browser/overlayWiring.test.ts`, one of them an
  unbounded `pnpm exec vitest run`. Repaired in-branch rather than filed.

**The repair that caused the next finding, recorded rather than hidden:** round 1's finding 5 was
closed by asserting `spawnBounded` already covered the fallback. Nobody had checked. That sentence
became round 2's finding 1. It is why the plan now carries a strictly-weaker-implementation pass over
every acceptance criterion, and a second pass asking whether any other rule or default in the same
design neutralizes each fixture — the second question is what the AC-6 defect turned out to be.

**Infra:** none. All five dispatches returned a verdict; no `no_verdict`, no reaped run, no out-dir
