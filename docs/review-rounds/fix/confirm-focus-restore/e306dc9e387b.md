The invariant underneath: **a task whose command cannot be red for its OWN work
is not a task.** Each round I fixed the named instances and the next round found
the survivors, which is the per-instance whack-a-mole the class-sweep rule warns
about. What actually closed it was asking what every remaining task's red
DEPENDS ON, and moving the three that depend on other tasks into a closeout
section: the e2e cases, the structural absence check, and the matrix. Six TDD
tasks remain.

Worth recording because it was not obvious to me: `docs/agents/writing-plans.md`
makes the transition audit MANDATORY, and I read that as "must be a task". It
requires the audit to exist and to run. An audit of coverage that the tasks
produce can only be red before them and green after, which spans tasks by
construction and cannot live inside one.

The plan stage also caught two substantive errors the spec stage had left
standing: Task 5's GREEN could not have worked, because the restore effect gates
on raw `ui === "idle"` while a refused revoke leaves `ui === "resolving"`; and
`block: "nearest"` (AC-3) appeared in a RED and vanished from its GREEN.

**Mechanizable:** declined: the recurring shape — a task marker whose `red=`
command cannot fail for that task's own change — is a plan-lint arm, and
`spec:lint --exec-red` already runs `red-state=live` commands. Extending it to
`authored` reds would need the arm to know which task produces which assertion,
which is the plan's semantics rather than its grammar. Under the process mint
freeze this is a documented limit on the plan-lint surface, not a queue row, and
it has not recurred across independent arcs.
