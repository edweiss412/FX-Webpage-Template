### The finding ratio, as a method

Classify every finding by **what it was against** — the shipped deliverable, or the scaffolding
attached to it (guard, comment, doc). Count per round. It takes two minutes and it answers a question
the total cannot: whether the review is still reviewing the artifact.

Measured across the four PRs this session shipped, all reviewed by the same reviewer against briefs
of the same shape, ten diff rounds and fifteen declared findings:

| PR | subject | rounds (declared findings) | vs DELIVERABLE | vs scaffolding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| #864 | ledger-kinds parity oracle + cheapness guard | r1(3) r2(2) r3(2) | **0** | 7 |
| #865 | 8 re-keyed ledger rows | r1(2) r2(0) | **0** | 2 (comment prose) |
| #866 | ceiling/budget pin + spec narrowing | r1(4) r2(2) r3(0) | **2** | 4 (docs/prose) |
| #867 | 2 strengthened test cases | r1(0) | 0 | — |

**Two of fifteen touched shipped behaviour, and both landed in round 1** (#866's bound that was not
delivered at the boundary, and its hand-written measured set). Every finding in every round 2, 3 and
4, on every arc, was against a guard, a comment or a doc.

The totals did not decay — 3, 2, 2 and 4, 2, 0 — while the deliverable rate went to zero after round
one and stayed there. **That is why a flat total is not evidence that review is still paying: the
scaffolding is inexhaustible and the deliverable is not.** An arc reading only the total sees a
review still producing findings and dispatches again.

**#867 is the control, and it should be read as one rather than as the arc that went well.** It had
no guard attached — its deliverable was two test cases — and it returned one round, APPROVE, zero
findings. It isolates the variable: what predicts the round count here is not reviewer behaviour and
not the number of rounds, it is *whether there is scaffolding to review*.

**The claim this supports is narrow, deliberately.** Round 1 is NOT sufficient — #866's two
round-1 findings were both real and one was a genuine defect in the author's own derivation. The
claim is only this: **once the ratio goes to zero for a round, the next round is very unlikely to
move it back, and that is the moment to check whether the review has changed subject.**

**What a reader should take from this arc:** the deliverable was right after round 1, and four rounds

### The instrument this arc produced, and the failure that forced it

A readiness reporter that reads the twelve required contexts BY NAME, in both vocabularies, and
splits failures three ways: **BLOCKING** (a required context), **known-benign** (a named row with a
recorded cause), and **UNEXPLAINED** (anything else non-required).

The split exists because of one moment on this PR. `unit-suite-db (6)` failed on
`_metaStripCommentsSingleSource` — a real required-context red, caused by a comment stripper this
arc had hand-rolled two commits earlier. It sat in the rollup **beside two failures that are
genuinely benign**: `budget`, the needs-coordinator left unsatisfiable by a cancelled harness run,
and `Vercel`, an account-level build rate limit that fails on `main` itself.

An instrument that lumps those together fails in both directions, and both were live:

- **Block on noise.** Treating "any FAILURE on the PR" as disqualifying would have held a ready PR
  behind two rows that can never go green while the rate limit holds.
- **Ship a red.** Having just been told to expect a benign `budget` failure, the cheap read is
  "failures present, all known" — which would have merged a required-context red.

Reading shard names before believing the aggregate is what separated them, and it is the same
discipline three other instruments needed tonight: a sharded job's rollup misrepresents its shards, a
cancelled run's status misrepresents its jobs, and a commit's aggregate status mixes check-runs with
commit statuses so it reports a failure no by-name required-check query can see.

**The generalization, which is what belongs in a corpus row rather than the tool:** a verdict
aggregated over a heterogeneous set is not a verdict about any member of it. Whenever a gate reads
one, the question to ask is which members it is summing — and whether any of them can fail for a
reason that has nothing to do with the thing being gated.
