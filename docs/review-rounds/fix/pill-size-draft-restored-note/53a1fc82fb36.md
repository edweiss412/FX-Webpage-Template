**Judgment (round 5):**

- **Both residual findings were census errors, and a census is exactly what a self-sweep is worst at.** My own sweep read each cited line and asked whether the sentence was true of it, which is why almost nothing else survived. What it could not catch were two claims about the *absence* or *extent* of things: "no committed fixture renders all three segments" (a jsdom unit test does; only the real-browser claim is true) and a list of a shared harness entry's consumers (I named a suite that merely scans its source, and missed a real-browser suite that drives it). Verifying a claim you can point at is tractable; verifying a claim about everything you did not point at needs a search, and I wrote both sentences from what I had already read rather than from disk. The rule that falls out: **a negative or enumerative claim gets its own `rg` over the tree, and the command goes in the commit.**
