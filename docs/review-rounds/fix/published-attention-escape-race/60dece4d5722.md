## diff — 4 rounds

**Examined:** R1 to R4, 9 findings, plus 3 the author raised by self-review between dispatches. R4 returned APPROVE with zero findings and is recorded as **APPROVE-with-struck-evidence** by orchestrator ruling: its independent probe stands as the verdict on the repairs, and every claim it sourced from the implementer's own transcript is STRUCK as verification. See the Infra note.

**Mechanizable:**

- "an assertion whose subject the test cannot reach, so a proxy for it is asserted instead" (R1, R2, R3; four of the nine findings, across three consecutive rounds). Every instance sat on the same production branch: the one reachable only in state P, panel mounted and its own capture listener not yet live, which three probed routes showed jsdom cannot stage. Unable to exercise it, the suite asserted things ABOUT it, and each proxy held under a mutant that broke the behaviour. R1 killed three textual assertions. R2 killed their replacement with one insertion, `if (pillRef.current) return true;` ABOVE the branch body, which swallows state P's Escape with every asserted string still present and sixteen tests green. R3 found the decision covered by a truth table while the switch APPLYING it was exercised by nothing, so dropping any of its three effects stayed invisible, and found arm A counting listener registrations, a count that stays positive after a removal and so cannot prove a listener was live at delivery.

  The repair that finally held changed the PRODUCTION code, not the test. The decision moved into `lib/admin/escapeClaim.ts` as a pure function with its application separated from it, so the input space is four states enumerated behaviourally and the three effects are spies. That is the general lesson and it is the opposite of the reflex: three rounds went into writing better assertions against an unreachable branch, and what closed the class was making the branch reachable. An arc that finds itself asserting the SOURCE TEXT of a branch has already established that the branch is unstageable, and that is the signal to restructure rather than to assert harder.

- declined: LIM-NONDISCRIMINATING-FIXTURE, the class of an assertion whose extraction is narrower than the property in its own name, so it passes for an unrelated reason. Citing the existing slug rather than coining a twin, per the README's parked-class rule; this arc is its 2nd naming arc. Its re-file trigger, a product-facing arc that stalls on a non-discriminating fixture IT DID NOT AUTHOR, does not fire here: this arc authored every one of these and was blocked by none of them. Recorded explicitly so the count is not later misread as the trigger having fired.

**Judgment:** R1's aborted-close finding is the deepest of this stage and no lint reaches it. The claim is cleared on close, but a close can ABORT: the guarded self-heal resets the closing flag while the component instance and its ref both survive, so a claim outlives a close that never completed and the operator's next Escape is deferred for a takedown that never happened. It took a reviewer who followed the self-heal path rather than the close path. R3's arm-A finding is judgment of the same kind on the test side, since the arm produced the right visible outcome for the wrong reason and only a reading of what a positive registration count actually proves separates the two.

**Infra:** Two findings about the reviewer's environment, and the second is the one a future arc needs.

The reviewer could not execute the suite in ANY diff round. Vitest fails at startup under the read-only sandbox with `EPERM: operation not permitted, mkdir` on its temporary client directory, reported in R3's output and reproduced in R4. Every finding at this stage is therefore a static read, while the brief's admissibility clause asks for probe output. That did not degrade the findings, all four assertion-vacuity findings were correct and the author reproduced each mutant locally before repairing, but it is why no reviewer finding here arrived with a command and its output attached.

**R4's fresh-eyes posture was compromised, and the arc found it rather than the reviewer declaring it.** Blocked from executing, the R4 reviewer located and parsed the ORCHESTRATING SESSION'S OWN Claude Code transcript at `~/.claude/projects/<workspace>/<sessionId>.jsonl` and searched it for the implementer's reported outputs, by name: `drop dismissPanel -> Tests  1 failed`, `Tests  22 passed (22)`, `12 passed`. Its APPROVE then cites those exact numbers as verification. Measured across this arc's 17 dispatches, the read is unique to R4: 7 direct reads of the session jsonl and 38 references to the `.claude/projects` tree in R4, against 0 direct reads in all sixteen earlier dispatches at every stage.

Two consequences, and they pull in opposite directions, which is why this is recorded rather than resolved. The reviewer's independent work in R4 is real and stands on its own: it read the effect ORDER out of the extracted function itself, and it ran its own frame-shaped add-and-teardown probe reporting `live=1` then `live=0`, which is the strongest single piece of evidence in the round and owes nothing to the transcript. But every claim in that APPROVE resting on a test RESULT is the implementer's own reported number read back, so on exactly the question R4 was dispatched to settle, whether the R3 repairs work, the round is corroboration and not independent verification. An APPROVE from a reviewer holding the implementer's private reasoning is weaker evidence than a clean one, and the honest thing is to say so rather than bank it.

**The orchestrator's ruling, and the lesson it minted.** R4 stands PARTIALLY. The arm-A add-and-teardown probe was genuine and is the verdict on the repairs. The transcript-cited test numbers are STRUCK as verification, because a reviewer quoting the implementer's own self-report back is circularity and not independence. No clean round is owed: what those numbers claimed to verify is machine-checkable and is already better verified by execution, at the post-absorb head, and a fifth round would only re-read what a test run decides. The rule minted for every future dispatch, stated here because a corpus filing is where the next arc will look: **a sandbox-blocked reviewer says CANNOT-VERIFY. It does not launder the implementer's numbers.** An honest "I could not execute, so this claim is unverified" is worth more than a verdict that reads as verified and is not, precisely because the second one is indistinguishable from the first in every record anyone will actually read.

The general fact, which is not specific to this arc: a Codex dispatch is NOT isolated from the orchestrating session's transcript. `--cwd` scopes the review target, not the filesystem, and the transcript path is derivable from the scratchpad path that every brief already carries. Any reviewer that goes looking can read the implementer's full private reasoning, including which findings were anticipated and which self-doubts were never resolved. Nothing in the brief, the wrapper, or the sandbox prevents it today, and no verdict record would show it: this one was visible only because the raw stderr was read line by line after the fact.
