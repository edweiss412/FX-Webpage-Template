**Judgment:** rounds 1 and 2 were the intended product of review. R1's two red findings could not have been caught mechanically and both mattered: (a) the diff paired a CI run's elapsed times with weights derived from a DIFFERENT revision — run 31989590619 is head `c5518dfab` at 29 enrolled surfaces while the branch base carries 30, because #832 enrolled `sameOriginServerAction` after that run — so an elapsed/weight comparison was drawn across two partitions that never coexisted; (b) the row's "first scheduled step" reasoned from the SHAPE of two mutation-survivor sites (a boundary comparison, an integer inside a string) to "plausibly equivalent, so ledger rows rather than tests", when both mutants change behavior — `budgetSeconds <= 0` widened to `< 0` makes a zero budget stop failing, which the line's own comment forbids, and `WARN_FRACTION * 100` becoming `* 101` prints `over 75.75%`. That row would have instructed the next reader to bless two genuine defects. Neither is reachable by any gate this repo has; both are exactly what a second reader is for.

**Mechanizable:** one shape, and it is the dominant cost of rounds 2-4. Every finding after R1 was the same defect: **a bounded observation written as a standing property.** R2 found a paragraph that claimed two items "reproduce cross-branch" and then said one of them was observed once. R3 found three sites asserting a timeout masked a surface on BOTH earlier runs when only one of the two runs contained the commit that stales that ledger (`git merge-base --is-ancestor fbfc04fdf 95c08e62f` exits 1, and that head still carries the pre-shift blob), plus a "one enrolment away" claim about a threshold the tree had already crossed unmeasured. R4 found two peers of a claim the implementer had itself softened one commit earlier.

That last one is the instructive failure, and it is charged here rather than to the reviewer. After R3, the implementer recognised the recurring shape and ran an unprompted class sweep (`8c69cd628`), which is the correct response to same-vector recurrence — but swept by **reading for the shape** and so repaired the two instances it happened to notice while leaving two more that used the single word `retired`. A `grep -i retire` over the diff would have found all of them in one pass and cost nothing. This is the "sweep to a derivation, not a longer list" rule failing in its most ordinary form: the sweep was real, it was just conducted as a re-read rather than as a mechanical enumeration over the token that names the class. Where a class has a lexical handle, the sweep owes a grep, and the grep belongs in the same commit as the first repair.

The general half is not mechanizable and should not be filed as though it were. "Does this sentence claim more than its evidence carries?" has no recognizer, and building one would be the parser-growth failure AGENTS.md already warns about. What IS mechanizable is narrower and already available: when a repair softens a claim, grep the diff for the words that claim carried.

declined: no ledger row. The half that is mechanizable is not a check but a step in an existing rule — the class-sweep rule already says sweep to a derivation, and a lexical class already implies its own grep; a row saying "remember to grep" is a restatement of AGENTS.md, not schedulable work. The half that would need building is a recognizer for "claims more than its evidence carries", which has no closable bound and is precisely the recognizer-growth shape the convergence criterion forbids. Filing either would breach the ledger admissibility bar (AGENTS.md, "Ledger filing bar", 2026-08-04): unprobed, with no reachable live surface, and a worst case that is a review round rather than a corruption. The probe that would change this is a frequency count of softening-repairs that left a lexical peer behind, across the review-rounds corpus — and that probe, not an entry, would be the first step if anyone picks it up.
