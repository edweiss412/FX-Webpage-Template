## spec — 5 rounds

**Examined:** 27 findings across rounds 1-5 (9, 8, 5, 4, 1), every one accepted, none argued down. The arc's
deliverable is one paragraph of README convention, a census script wired into no gate, a probe record,
and two ledger edits. No product code. The question this record answers is why a deliverable that
small took the full round budget.

**The shape, and it is not the guard ratchet.** The usual four-round arc grows a recognizer one input
family per round. This one did the opposite twice on purpose: round 2's central repair DELETED a claim
("fourteen of fifteen records are bound") rather than improving the instrument that failed to support
it, and round 3's did the same to "zero true positives". The census script is 280 lines and ended the
arc with fewer assertions than it started with. What actually consumed the rounds was that **the
deliverable's own subject is claim-versus-evidence discipline, and every round found the arc violating
it in a new place.**

That is worth stating plainly rather than as an excuse: an arc about numbers that go stale because a
person carried them shipped, in three consecutive drafts, a number a person had carried. Round 1: the
record claimed one command produced every figure in it, when `git` commands and a hand classification
produced several. Round 2: an anchor screen that only detects the ABSENCE of an anchor was read as a
certificate that fourteen records were bound. Round 3: an exact "zero true positives" that required a
per-figure binding judgment round 2 had already established the arc does not make — and my own round-2
sweep commit wrote "seventeen" where its table said fifteen. Round 4: four instances of text that had
outlived its claim, two of them alive only because a round-3 replace silently no-op'd on an
indentation mismatch and nobody grepped to confirm it landed. Round 5: one sentence saying "the one
such record" where the census prints two rows, the second excluded by a rule the prose never stated.

**The decay is the part worth carrying forward.** 9 → 8 → 5 → 4 → 1, with no design finding after
round 3 and none at all in rounds 4 and 5 — every late finding was the arc's own prose drifting from
the arc's own evidence. That profile says the design converged early and the DOCUMENTS took three
more rounds to catch up with it.

**Mechanizable:** three of the twenty-seven, all three in the instrument rather than the prose.

declined: all three are defects in a probe script wired into no gate, and filing repo-wide lint arms
off a throwaway apparatus's bugs is the process-facing inflation the 2026-08-18 mint bar exists to
stop. Each is fixed at its site; the shapes are recorded below so they are known rather than filed.

1. **`TREE_BINDING` was missing its `g` flag**, so `String.match(...).length` printed 0 or 1 for every
   record. Records carrying six phrases read 1 for two full rounds. The general rule — a regex used
   with `.match` for a COUNT must carry `g` — is real, and nothing in this repo checks it. One site,
   now fixed; a repo-wide arm is more than a two-round cost in one probe script supports.
2. **The tokenizer-sensitivity column never reconciled.** It counted numeric matches inside each
   excised span instead of the drop in the population, so 1709 − 540 ≠ 1214. Now true by construction
   here — removal IS the measured drop — and the invariant does not generalize past scripts that
   print an exclusion breakdown, of which this is the only one.
3. **Reported line numbers were indices into a fence-stripped array**, wrong for 19 of 23 emitted
   locations. Any script that strips regions before reporting positions has this available. The check
   worth knowing is that a printed `file:line` resolves to the quoted text; it is not worth a gate
   against a single apparatus.

**Judgment:** the other twenty-four, and they do not have a mechanical form. "Does this sentence claim
more than its producer supports" is the same judgment the arc concluded is not mechanizable for
figures in documents — which is the arc's own finding turned on the arc. A cheaper path existed and
was not taken: **state the instrument's limits BEFORE writing conclusions from it.** The script now
carries a header saying what it can and cannot decide; had that been written when the script was, the
"fourteen of fifteen" and "zero true positives" drafts would not have been written at all. Two rounds,
recoverable by ten minutes of writing the limits first.

**Infra:** none at this stage. No spec dispatch was reaped, no `no_verdict`, no wrapper fault. Five
spec dispatches, five verdicts.

**What would have compressed this.** Not a better reviewer and not a tighter brief — rounds 1-3 each
found real defects and the briefs stated bound, fence and probe domain from round 1. The compression
is upstream: an instrument whose limits are written down at the same time as its output cannot have
its output over-read, and over-reading it was the class behind 24 of 27 findings.

## diff — 4 rounds

**Examined:** 8 findings across rounds 1-4 (2, 2, 3, 1), every one accepted, none argued down, plus a
round-1 dispatch that died `total_timeout` before emitting a verdict and does not count. Every finding
was one class: **a claim asserting more than its producer supports.** That is this arc's deliverable
turned on the arc, for the second stage running — the spec stage's record above says the same thing
about its own 27.

**Why one class produced four rounds when the spec stage's found it too.** The spec rounds read the
arc's conclusions. The diff rounds read its *producers*, and those are a different population: a
sentence can be true, reviewed, and approved at spec time while the command printed beneath it cannot
establish it. Round 2 is the clean example. `That branch is gone` was true, had survived five spec
rounds, and sat above `git ls-remote … | wc -l` printing `0` — which a DNS failure also prints, with
the pipeline exiting 0. The conclusion needed no repair; the proof did.

**The rounds did not find the same instance in new places, they found the same shape in new layers.**
Round 1: prose figures stale against the JSONL that produces them. Round 2: a shell pipeline that
masks its own upstream failure, and a grep counting diff lines read as counting occurrences. Round 3:
an accept-set (a tag) wider than the property it claims, a count stated without the exclusion that
makes it true, and — the one worth carrying — the round-1 repair's own header, which said every count
came from the JSONL when the JSONL produces only counts, not the classifications built on them. Round
4: a code comment stating 43 epoch-ms where its producer yields 43 all-digit of which 40 are epoch-ms.

**Judgment:** all eight. None has a mechanical form, and the arc's own §4 measurement is why: the
sketched gate for exactly this class reds 23 times on this corpus, at least 15 on lines that are not
figures, and misses the one record the anchor screen flags. A recognizer for "does this sentence claim
more than its producer supports" is the thing this arc measured and declined, so filing lint arms off
its own review findings would ship the unreliability it was chartered to remove.

**Mechanizable:** none, and one near-miss worth naming rather than filing. Round 2's defect —
`cmd | wc -l` where `0` is the conclusion — is the one shape here with a real grammar. A sweep found
its only other instances are two pre-existing `BACKLOG-archive.md` lines this branch does not touch,
so an arm would ship with zero live customers beyond the site already fixed. Under the 2026-08-18 mint
bar that is a documented limit, not a queue entry: the incident is real but the population is one.

**Infra:** one. The round-1 dispatch ran 3060s and was SIGTERM'd on `total_timeout` with no verdict,
having spent the budget hand-reading `BACKLOG.md` to re-derive counts a committed script prints in
seconds. The re-dispatch added a paragraph telling the reviewer to run the census instead, and every
round since has finished inside its window. Cost: one dead dispatch, about 51 minutes. The general
lesson is cheap — a review brief for a diff carrying an instrument should point the reviewer at the
instrument, because the reviewer's default is to read.

**What would have compressed this.** Writing the producers down beside the claims, once, at the time
the claims were written. The spec stage's record already concluded that and the diff stage proves it
was not acted on: the round-1 repair added a binding header, and round 3 found that header itself
over-claiming. A binding written after the fact is another claim needing its own check. The rule that
survives both stages is narrower and harder than "cite your source" — **a producer is not a citation,
it is a command whose output you have actually compared against the sentence.**
